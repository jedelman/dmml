# Section VII: a fourth citation posture, and a real fan-in shape (2026-08-25)

`dmml/examples/benjamin_section_vii.rs` models Section VII's two moves.

First, a fourth citation posture in this essay, distinct from all three
prior ones (Valery: endorsed; Gance's Section II quote: hedged-but-
sympathetic; Riegl/Wickhoff: correct-but-incomplete). Here, four external
theorists — Gance (a SECOND, different quote — the hieroglyphs
comparison, not the "will make films" enthusiasm from Section II),
Severin-Mars, Arnoux, Werfel — are cited NEGATIVELY, as instances of a
diagnosed error (forcing ritual/sacred vocabulary onto film to justify
calling it "art," when the real question — did photography's invention
transform art's entire nature — was never even raised). Modeled as a real
fan-in: one diagnosis fact, consumed independently by four witness
commits that never cite each other, each just consuming the diagnosis
plus its own quoted source — checked, not merely described, at consumes
count 2 each.

Gance's two appearances (Section II's and this section's) are modeled as
genuinely separate facts — different (uri, cid), different claims,
different postures — not the same fact reused with a different gloss.
Worth remembering going forward: an author can appear more than once in
this essay's citation graph with a different relationship to Benjamin's
argument each time, and the model needs to keep those separate rather
than collapsing a person into one fixed posture.

Second, Benjamin's own counter-move: L'Opinion publique and The Gold Rush
had already appeared when these speculations were published — the sacred
framing was already empirically outdated even as spoken. Modeled as a
rebuttal commit consuming all four witnesses at once (checked: consumes
count 4), matching the text's own move of undercutting all of them
together rather than singling one out.
