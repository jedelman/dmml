## Did two sources actually produce more, or just more words?

Genuinely more, on the concrete evidence: agents cite **both authors
by name, correctly, throughout** -- Athena invoking Adorno's "amusement
... prolongation of work" against Dionysus's festival; Athena later
quoting Adorno's own letter ("anything but good and revolutionary")
back at Dionysus's closing claim about cult-forming laughter; multiple
turns explicitly crediting one author's structure while disputing the
other's conclusion (Apollo: "Adorno wins his argument and Benjamin wins
his, because domination's reach and freedom's distance are the same
fact measured twice"). That is real cross-source synthesis, not
side-by-side commentary -- something structurally impossible with only
one source in the room, which is exactly the failure mode Jason
diagnosed.

**One real cost, not hidden**: citation discipline was measurably worse
this run. 3 of 20 agent turns landed with zero verified citations
(versus 0 of 20 in the single-source, same-round-count run) -- doubling
the anchor pool from 8 to 16 gave the model more surface area to
mismatch a subject/predicate pair while copying. Worth weighing before
adding a third source: more real material to react to bought more
substantive engagement, but at a real, measured cost to structural
citation reliability.
