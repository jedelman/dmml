# The triple: Gramsci, Federici, Ostrom (2026-08-27)

Jason's follow-up after the Power Explained bibliography survey: grep
across the book's 1020 gesture files put Gramsci (26 citations),
Federici (22), and Ostrom (17) at the top of the book's real intellectual
debt, each with a `G-DP-*` dramatis-personae profile in that repo.
"That's the triple! go for it." Same debate/reflect/consensus/explain
pipeline proven on Benjamin/Adorno tonight, run against three real
sources that were never in dialogue with each other historically.

## The anchors

24 real anchors (8 per thinker), verified via live web search before
building on them, same discipline as every other source tonight: exact
quotes where a search turned up verbatim text (Gramsci's interregnum
passage, his "spontaneous consent" formulation, Ostrom's exact wording
for graduated sanctions and nested enterprises), clearly-sourced
paraphrases elsewhere (Federici's book has no single quotable sentence
for most of its claims -- the paraphrases are attributed to the real,
specific arguments the secondary sources confirmed, not invented).
Sources: Gramsci's *Prison Notebooks* (1971 Selections translation),
Federici's *Caliban and the Witch* (2004), Ostrom's *Governing the
Commons* (1990).

## What the debate actually did

Same four Olympian personas, same model (z-ai/glm-5.3), same
tool-call-disciplined DMML turns, five rounds plus reflection. The real
question posed to them: does Ostrom's institutional durability answer
Gramsci's hegemony, or does Federici's account of the body's enclosure
reach somewhere no design principle can?

The debate did not stay where it started. Athena opened with a clean
division of labor (Ostrom governs resources, Federici's terrain is the
body, the two never meet); by round 2 that division was dismantled --
first by Dionysus ("there are no withdrawals of mere things"), then by
the group converging on collective choice (who sits in the rule-making)
as the real load-bearing hinge, then by a genuine, hard-won concession
from all four that ecstasy is not outside the war of position but its
fuel -- the witch hunt itself was a counterfeit festival. The debate's
last two rounds turned on a real disagreement that was never resolved:
whether a "constitutionalized" festival (feast days on the books,
funded, defensible) is a defended commons or, as Artemis put it, "a wolf
in a paddock" -- and whether wildness given any function, even a
protective one, has already been enclosed.

## Citation discipline: worse than the two-source run, a real finding

**4 of 24 turns landed with zero verified citations** (round 1: athena,
artemis; round 2: artemis; round 6/reflection: dionysus) -- worse than
`pantheon_olympians.rs`'s perfect 8/8 on the two-source Benjamin/Adorno
run. 36 total citation attempts were dropped as non-existent across the
24 turns (subject/predicate typos, wrong-cid guesses, and a few outright
hallucinated node names), roughly 1.5 dropped citations per turn even
where at least one real one landed. The likely cause, worth naming
honestly rather than glossing over: three sources with 24 anchors gives
the model more real (cid, subject, predicate) combinations to misremember
than two sources with 16 did, and the debate's own accumulating log grew
correspondingly larger and harder to copy from exactly. This is a real,
useful data point for the paper's citation-reliability discussion: more
source material did not just add content, it degraded citation
mechanics measurably, even though none of it broke the *argument's* real
cumulative structure (every dropped citation still left at least one
verified one standing, except where a turn genuinely floated free).

## Consensus: reached, but not on the first try

Unlike the Benjamin/Adorno run (unanimous on Athena's very first draft),
this synthesis took **2 ratification rounds**. Artemis amended the
initial draft specifically to restore her own retreat ("Ostrom's
machinery is the witch hunt's equipment" narrowed to "the enclosure of
the unscheduled") and Athena/Artemis's unresolved credit-arithmetic
dispute, both of which the first draft had flattened. All four accepted
the amended version unanimously in round 2. A real amendment cycle, not
a rubber stamp -- consistent with this being a harder synthesis problem
(three sources, more open threads) than the two-source run.

## The writers room: holds up again

All four explained the ratified synthesis in plain language to an
outsider, forbidden from using the debate's own coinages ("the
zoo-wolf," "minting rapture," "the war of position made structural")
without defining them first. All four pass, each reaching for a
different concrete anchor -- Athena and Artemis both reach for a
community-garden/shared-pasture image but diverge sharply in tone
(Athena constructive, Artemis retaining her huntress register even in
translation); Apollo stays structural but names his own metaphor as his
own ("my metaphor, and I'll own it"); Dionysus opens "pour something"
and stays in character to the last line while still being clear about
what a "counterfeit festival" is. All four preserve the real unresolved
disagreement (whether housed wildness is still wildness) rather than
manufacturing a tidier close for the outside reader.
