Two things worth having built rather than just noted in prose:

1. The exhibition-value gradient (elk → temple statue → portrait bust →
   mosaic/fresco → painting → mass → symphony) is entirely WITHIN the
   ritual era — none of these facts cite reproduction technology at all
   (consumes count 0 for all of them). Mechanical reproduction accelerates
   a gradient that was already running, it doesn't invent the exhibition
   pole. Easy to miss if you only remember the ritual→reproduction jump
   from earlier sections.

2. The prehistoric/today mirror ("by the absolute emphasis on its cult
   value, it was, first and foremost, an instrument of magic... in the
   same way today, by the absolute emphasis on its exhibition value...")
   is modeled as two commits sharing the identical core predicate set
   (`dominantValue`, `recognizedFunction`) on two different subjects,
   both consuming the same quantity-into-quality fact as their shared
   licensing premise. Checked structurally, not just described: the
   predicate sets actually match. One real asymmetry surfaced by the
   check itself: today's side gets an extra `exemplar` fact (photography
   and film) that prehistory has no analogue for — Benjamin doesn't claim
   prehistory named its own future medium, so the mirror isn't perfectly
   symmetric, and the model shouldn't pretend otherwise.
