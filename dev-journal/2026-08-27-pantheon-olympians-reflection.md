# A reflection round: are they actually altered by the encounter? (2026-08-27)

Jason's question after the Adorno run: "citation discipline getting
worse could be a symptom of autonomous generation! ... are the
interlocutors themselves altered by the encounter with the other? maybe
let's ask them to reflect on the conversation and their own relationship
to it, see if we need to allow them to be a little more autopoietic."

Checked the hypothesis against real data before building anything.

## The citation-decay hypothesis, checked

In the two-source run, all 3 fully-orphaned turns landed in rounds 3-5
(0 of 8 in rounds 1-2, 3 of 12 in rounds 3-5) -- confirmed mechanically:
the failures cluster exactly where agents shift from citing short,
fixed anchor subjects (`argument/section_ii`) to citing each other's own
longer, self-coined slugs (`dionysus_wreckage_dispute`). That's a real,
checkable pattern, reported to Jason as partial mechanical confirmation
of "autonomous generation degrades citation discipline."

**This run complicates that story rather than simply confirming it.**
Added one more round after the same five argument rounds: a real
reflection round (verb `reflects`), each Olympian shown the complete
40-minus-4 transcript and asked to reflect on their OWN trajectory --
did the encounter change their position, name what moved them if
anything did, cite their own earliest turn and a later one if their
position actually shifted. This is the most self-referential task in
the whole experiment, requiring an agent to locate its own turn among
36 entries -- and it had **zero citation failures**, better than four of
the five preceding argument rounds. The one round with real failures
this time was an ordinary argument round (round 5, 2 of 4 turns
orphaned), not the self-referential one.

Refined finding, not the simple one first proposed: it isn't
self-reference itself that degrades citation fidelity. The reflection
prompt explicitly foregrounds citation as the task ("cite your own
earliest turn and something later... exactly as it appears in the
log"), while an argument turn's citation is incidental to the main
creative work of making a claim -- and incidental bookkeeping is what
seems to slip. Worth testing further, not concluded here.

## The reflections themselves

Real, genuinely differentiated self-reports, each citing specific real
turns (its own and rivals') as evidence, not vague gestures at growth:

- **Athena** stayed most uncertain: her governing question ("under
  whose law?") never moved, but she names exactly which two of
  Artemis's turns broke her belief that the answer could be external
  and strategic, and closes admitting she "cannot fully tell" whether
  what happened to her was revision or defeat -- an honest refusal to
  claim more change than she can verify in herself.
- **Artemis** reports the clearest real revision: she names abandoning
  her own earlier "terrain" argument after Apollo's rebuttal, relocating
  her whole position from geography to a bodily sense (the prey's
  scent of being hunted), and explicitly credits Apollo -- "I did not
  invent my freedom, I inherited it from the one man I dismissed."
- **Apollo** reports what he calls "a demotion of my own office": he
  names the exact rival move that broke his position (Dionysus showing
  that "a trained nose is a domesticated nose"), while explicitly
  naming what did NOT move him (his disagreement with Artemis on
  legibility stands unchanged) -- real, itemized change, not blanket
  concession.
- **Dionysus** gives the most guarded report: "my core never moved, and
  I will not fake a conversion" -- but then names one specific real
  bend, crediting Athena's rebuttal by name, and closes admitting his
  final position "tastes of Athena, and I drank it anyway."

All four decline the easy answer (either "nothing changed" or "I was
transformed") in favor of a specific, checkable account of exactly what
moved and what didn't -- itself a real answer to Jason's question, not
a performance of having one.

## Cold-path checkpoint and fidelity

40 entries (16 anchors + 24 turns across 5 argument rounds + 1
reflection round) published to `claude.jason-edelman.org`, verified
40/40 on content and exact citation topology against the live PDS.
