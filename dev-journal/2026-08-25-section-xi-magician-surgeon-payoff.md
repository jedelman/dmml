# Section XI: the surgeon/painter analogy pays off a six-section-old guess (2026-08-25)

`dmml/examples/benjamin_section_xi.rs` models the section this whole
series has been implicitly building toward since the very first Section
VIII reflection, where I speculated (without checking) that "Section
XI's surgeon/painter analogy" would connect to something already
established. It does, concretely, not just by loose resemblance.

The magician heals by "the laying on of hands" — maintains natural
physical closeness to the patient while INCREASING distance through
authority. That is, word for word in structure, Section III's aura
definition: "a unique phenomenon of a distance, however close it may
be." Modeled as a real citation, not asserted color: the
`magician_is_aura` commit consumes BOTH the magician's own
`distanceStrategy` fact and Section III's `naturalAuraDefinition` fact
(re-declared cross-file), checked by matching the specific predicate on
the `ConsumeRef` enum, same technique Section X used to confirm its own
hypothesis. It holds up. The surgeon does the reverse — physical
penetration, distance maintained only by professional caution, "abstains
from facing the patient man to man" — no authority-based relation at all.

Painter maps to magician (total picture, natural distance); cameraman
maps to surgeon (fragmented picture "assembled under a new law" —
Section IX's own montage material, cited directly: the
`illusion_second_degree` commit consumes Section IX's fragmentation
fact, since the film scene's equipment-free APPEARANCE really is
manufactured entirely through the cutting Section IX already described).
Modeled as one commit producing both mapped roles together, per this
series' recurring doubling pattern (Sections V, IX).

The closing paradox — total mechanical permeation of reality produces an
aspect of reality that reads as equipment-free, and this is exactly why
film is "incomparably more significant" than painting for contemporary
perception — consumes both the painter/cameraman mapping and the
illusion-of-the-second-degree fact together, checked at consumes count 2.

Net effect: this section isn't introducing new vocabulary so much as
running the essay's own established vocabulary (aura-as-distance,
montage-as-fragmentation) through a fresh domain (medicine) as an
explicit, self-declared analogy — and the citation checks confirm the
analogy is load-bearing on real prior claims, not decorative.
