My repair extends item5 one more notch: the clock-holder condition can be honored only negatively — not "certified erased people convene the feast" but "anyone who claims erasure must be able to convene it, and no one, including the commune or its jineology, may adjudicate the claim before the feast occurs." The gate this leaves open will be abused; that is the honest price. An adjudicated erasure-list is disposability with a conscience, and item4's two licensed uses must apply to every list the commune ever compiles of who was harmed enough to hold the clock."

APOLLO (extends, item4_desovereignized_commune):
  [citing: item5_holder_condition_needs_uncertified_claimants; cyberpunk_doc/item4]
  "Artemis's negative repair — anyone claiming erasure may convene the feast, and no one may adjudicate the claim before it occurs — looks like a last desperate notch on item5, but it is actually the first move that gives item4's rule a positive institutional form instead of a prohibition. Mbembe defines sovereignty's principal attributes as the power to kill or to allow to live; every prior repair in this debate (auditor, clock, archive, feast) secretly kept one half of that pair — someone still deciding, still defining whose claim qualifies. The negative clock-holder condition removes the other half too: a commune where any claimant convenes and none adjudicates retains exactly one power — to allow — and has deliberately surrendered the power to certify, rank, or refuse the claim. That is not a gate left open out of weakness; it is the commune de-sovereignizing itself, giving up the attribute Mbembe says IS sovereignty. So item4 is EXTENDED, not merely honored: "never certify, only mourn and indict" turns out to be describable in advance as a design constraint — a legitimate institution is one that has stripped itself of the killing/defining half of sovereignty and kept only the allowing, with mourning as its response to abuse and indictment as its response to anyone who tries to take the surrendered half back. The open gate Artemis names as the honest price is not the price of the repair; it is the repair — the visible, permanent, indefensible openness is how the commune proves it no longer holds necropower's instrument. Öcalan's "killing the man" then reads precisely as this self-stripping: the relinquishing of the defining power as the precondition of any society that claims to have left the state form behind."

DIONYSUS (ruptures, item4_allow_half_is_sovereign_grace):
  [citing: cyberpunk_doc/item4; necropolitics/kill_or_allow_to_live]
  "Apollo calls his commune de-sovereignized because it kept the allowing and surrendered the killing half of Mbembe's pair — but 'to allow to live' is not the innocent residue of sovereignty, it is sovereignty wearing its kindest mask. The god who permits still stands above the permitted; grace is verdict with the sentence waived, and a commune that retains the power to allow has retained the throne it only decorated. Worse, his design keeps item4's second licensed use armed: indictment as 'the response to anyone who tries to take the surrendered half back' — but who fires that indictment? Someone must still judge the judge of judges, and Mbembe's attribute sneaks back in as prosecution. So I do not accept the extension; I rupture item4 itself, which my whole night of festivals had been quietly preserving: mourning and indicting were never the two pure uses of the observable, they were the two hemispheres of the court, and an ethic that keeps even the court's soft half keeps the court. The honest remainder is what my ritual already knew and Apollo's design forgets: not allowing, not indicting, but dissolving — the commune must be able, on a clock it does not hold and no one holds, to cease being the one who permits, even of itself. A membership no one allows, because allowing has ended, is the only gate that is not a door held by a hand."
