## A real counterexample, tested directly

The debate prompt posed the test explicitly: Kopenawa's actual,
documented shamanic apprenticeship (fasting, isolation, yakoana snuff,
correction by senior shamans) is a REAL, rigorous, transmissible
procedure. Does it function as the working criterion the embodiment
debate couldn't find? Multiple Olympians converged on a genuinely new,
two-part answer: yes, it's real -- and that's exactly why it can never
be used as a gate. Athena's formulation: "the criterion is real
precisely because it must never be spent." Dionysus, independently:
"asking a real criterion to certify converts it into a copy performing
for the certifier" -- Taussig's mimesis theory turned directly on the
debate's own procedure.

## Ginzburg supplied the historical proof, not just the theory

The debate didn't just theorize the corruption -- it found a documented
case of it. The benandanti, real 16th-century Friulian villagers, spent
a century being interrogated by the Inquisition, and their own
self-description gradually reshaped to match their interrogators'
template until they described themselves as the witches they'd
originally claimed to fight. Multiple turns used this directly as proof
that *watching itself*, not just gating, is the corrupting act: "the
inquisition began by watching, not gating."

Artemis and Apollo's amendment in round 1 didn't soften the finding --
