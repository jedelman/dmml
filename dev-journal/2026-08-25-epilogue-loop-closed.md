# The Epilogue: the loop closes (2026-08-25)

`dmml/examples/benjamin_epilogue.rs` is the last file in the section-by-
section Benjamin series, and it carries the biggest prediction made
across the whole session: does the Epilogue's political conclusion
genuinely consume the Preface's own stipulated vocabulary-stance fact,
closing the loop back to the essay's very first move? **Confirmed** —
Check 6, the closing claim ("Communism responds by politicizing art")
genuinely `consumes` the Preface's `vocabularyStance` fact, matched on
the specific predicate. The Preface's opening political stipulation
("concepts... completely useless for the purposes of Fascism") and the
Epilogue's closing political verdict are one continuous argument, not
two separate political gestures bookending an otherwise aesthetic-
theoretical essay in between.

Two smaller predictions confirmed the same way: "through gas warfare the
aura is abolished in a new way" genuinely consumes Section II's own
`aura` fact (Check 4) — the term coined in Section II reappears at its
most extreme possible case, gas warfare erasing even the last vestige of
what a human presence could mean. And "the consummation of l'art pour
l'art" genuinely consumes Section IV's l'art-pour-l'art fact (Check 5) —
Fascism's war-aesthetics isn't a fresh coinage, it's Section IV's own
defensive formation (art pour art, arising to answer a sensed crisis)
reaching its most monstrous possible endpoint.

Also modeled, and worth its own note: the "Führer cult" is checked to
consume Section X's movie-star cult fact directly (Check 1) — Benjamin's
political vocabulary and his commercial-culture vocabulary turn out, once
built, to be describing the SAME manufactured-cult structure: something
that borrows devotion's emotional form while being a "phony," industry-
or state-manufactured substitute for real cult value. Marinetti is cited
at length in a citation posture this series hasn't needed before —
content treated as monstrous and rejected, but the manifesto's clarity
explicitly endorsed as diagnostic evidence "dialecticians should accept"
— neither straightforwardly endorsed nor diagnosed-as-simply-wrong, a
genuine third option.

This closes the section-by-section Benjamin series: Preface through
Epilogue, sixteen files, every major citation posture this project
tracked (endorsed, hedged, correct-and-incomplete-and-licensing,
correct-and-narrow-and-defended, negatively diagnosed, hostile-but-
structurally-correct, correct-but-dismissed-as-commonplace,
content-rejected-but-clarity-valued) checked against real interpreter
output rather than asserted in prose, and — the thing that made this
series worth doing rather than just reading the essay twice — a set of
real, cross-section predictions made in conversation and then confirmed
or corrected under an actual `consumes` check, several sections apart
each time, all the way to the essay's own closing sentence.
