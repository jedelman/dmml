## Claim 1 — Deterritorialization/reterritorialization, general accuracy

**Claim:** "Deterritorialization" (and its pair "reterritorialization") is a
real, well-established D&G technical term, used across *Anti-Oedipus* and
*A Thousand Plateaus*, meaning roughly: a flow (of desire, matter, signs,
capital) breaking free from a coded/territorialized structure, which may
then get recoded ("reterritorialized") onto a new structure.

**Grade: ✅ Verified**

**Sources found:**
- The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's entry on Gilles Deleuze
  (`plato.stanford.edu/entries/deleuze/`) states directly: capitalism is
  "the radical decoding and deterritorialization of the material flows"
  that earlier social formations "had zealously coded on the earth or the
  body of the despot" — a primary-adjacent, citable philosophy-reference
  source confirming the coded-flow / freed-flow structure of the concept.
- A Sage Reference *Encyclopedia of Political Theory* entry on
  "Deterritorialization" (`sk.sagepub.com`) independently confirms: D&G use
  the term "to indicate the fracturing of and freeing from repressive
  fixations and despotic arrangements of a certain milieu, be it
  conceptual, social, affective or linguistic," and specifically notes the
  term and its pair are not opposites but mutually constitutive ("the two
  words are in and of each other"), matching the reterritorialization half
  of the claim precisely: in despotic empires, flows are "deterritorialized
  ... and then immediately 'reterritorialized' on the body of the despot,"
  and capitalism "frees (deterritorializes) materiality and human
  interaction from a hierarchical overcoding, but then also despotically
  recodes, reterritorializes."
- Both sources confirm the concept spans both books (introduced in
  *Anti-Oedipus*, 1972, elaborated as "the cutting edge of an assemblage"
  in *A Thousand Plateaus*, 1980), consistent with what the draft already
  cites for those two texts.

**Assessment:** This is a well-established, accurately stated D&G term at
exactly the level of generality the co-author's proposal uses. No
correction needed. As with the prior two passes, pull an exact pincite
from a paginated edition before submission rather than relying on
encyclopedia paraphrase.

## Claim 2 — "Agrammatical"/"agrammaticality" as a Deleuze(-Guattari) technical term

**Claim to check:** Is "agrammatical"/"agrammaticality" actually a term
Deleuze or Deleuze & Guattari use in a technical, load-bearing way, and is
there any *existing* D&G connection between it and deterritorialization
specifically, or would that connection be the paper's own synthesis?

**Grade: ✅ Verified as a real, load-bearing D&G term — stronger than the
prompt's own worry suggested, but with a real complication about which text
carries it**

**Findings — the term is real and used in more than one place, by both
solo Deleuze and by Deleuze & Guattari together:**

1. **In *A Thousand Plateaus* itself** (co-authored, not solo Deleuze), in
   the "Postulates of Linguistics" plateau: D&G discuss "atypical" or
   "agrammatical" expressions — the example repeatedly attested across
   independent sources is E.E. Cummings' "he danced his did" — as
   functioning as **tensors**. A quote surfaced independently and
   consistently across multiple searches: *"The atypical expression
   constitutes a cutting edge of deterritorialization of language, playing
   the role of tensor... it causes language to tend toward the limit of
   its elements, forms, or notions, toward a near side or a beyond of
   language."* This is attributed to the Massumi translation, around p.99.
   I was not able to fetch the primary PDF text directly (both the
   libcom.org and English-department mirror PDFs returned as
   non-extractable scanned/compressed streams to the fetch tool), so this
   quote is corroborated by convergent secondary reporting (a
   Rhuthmos.eu academic article and independent web search results
   describing the same passage in matching language) rather than a
   direct primary read — flagged as the honest limit of this pass, same
   caveat the rhizome pass applied to its own page-cited quotes.
2. **In Deleuze's solo *Critique et clinique* (English: *Essays Critical
   and Clinical*)**, specifically the Bartleby/Melville-adjacent material
   and "He Stuttered," Deleuze quotes linguist Nicolas Ruwet on
   agrammatical formulas as "a limit or a tensor" beyond ordinary
   grammatical variation — confirmed via a UC Berkeley TRANSIT journal
   article (Scherübl) that quotes the French directly: *"qu'on peut
   supposer une série de variables grammaticales ordinaires, dont la
   formule agrammaticale serait comme la limite"* (*Critique et Clinique*,
   p.90).

**On the deterritorialization connection specifically:** this is the one
place I can report a genuine, existing D&G textual link, not just a
plausible-sounding synthesis — **provided the ATP passage is the one
cited.** The ATP quote above states the connection explicitly and in D&G's
own words: the atypical/agrammatical expression "constitutes a cutting edge
of **deterritorialization of language**." The Berkeley TRANSIT article
makes the same link explicit for the Deleuze-solo material too, glossing
agrammaticality as leaving "the common structure of codified language...
the standard code of communication" — but that gloss is the *scholar's*
synthesis of Deleuze's essay, not a direct Deleuze quote using the word
"deterritorialization" in that specific passage the way ATP's own text
does.

**Honest strength assessment (per the prompt's specific ask):**
Agrammaticality is **not** as thin or marginal as "Deleuze's own occasional
usage in one essay about literary style" — it appears in *A Thousand
Plateaus* itself, in a properly co-authored, canonical plateau
("Postulates of Linguistics"), doing real conceptual work (tensors,
pragmatics, the critique of language as a homogeneous system) that the
book's argument leans on. That said, it is also **not** as load-bearing or
central to D&G's overall apparatus as deterritorialization itself, rhizome,
or desiring-production — it is one worked example (tensors, agrammatical
expressions) within one plateau's argument about linguistics, not a
recurring structural concept invoked across the whole two-book *Capitalism
and Schizophrenia* project the way deterritorialization is. The fair
characterization for the paper: agrammaticality is a real, ATP-native D&G
term with a genuine, D&G-stated tie to deterritorialization — solidly
citable, not a stretch — but it should be introduced as a specific,
narrower mechanism *within* deterritorialization (how a coded system's
formal grammar itself can be pushed to its limit and exceeded) rather than
implied to be as universally central a concept as deterritorialization is
on its own. The paper's proposed use (a new self-declared predicate
expanding DMML's grammar = "agrammatic," deterritorializing the grammar's
own closed vocabulary) is a **structural analogy the paper is drawing**,
not a move D&G make themselves (they never discuss anything resembling a
formal grammar/vocabulary being extended by a new predicate) — that
extension should be labeled explicitly as the paper's own synthesis, the
same discipline the rhizome pass required for the git/blockchain
comparison.

## Claim 3 — Does Simondon's individuation require conscious co-presence, or does the physical register (crystallization) individuate without anything like presence/consciousness?

**Claim to check:** Does Simondon's own framework span physical,
biological, psychic, and collective registers as structurally analogous —
in a way that makes "a computational runtime event, not a face-to-face
meeting, still counts as individuation" a plausible extension rather than a
category error?

**Grade: ✅ Verified (with one clearly labeled gap: no source directly
states "consciousness is not required" as a Simondon quote — that specific
phrasing is a reasonable inference from the physical-register material,
not something Simondon says in so many words)**

**Sources found:**
- An Epoché Magazine piece on Simondon's process of individuation
  confirms crystal genesis is Simondon's own worked example of **physical**
  individuation: "physical individuation as a case of the resolution of a
  metastable system," with crystal growth serving "as both an example of
  physical individuation, and as an analogy to understand how individuated
  systems form" more generally. The same source distinguishes vital
  (biological) individuation from physical individuation by *ongoing*
  resolution and transformation versus reliance on a "predefined physical
  structure such as that in crystal growth" — i.e., the registers are
  treated as variations on one process, differentiated by degree/kind of
  ongoing-ness, not as unrelated phenomena.
- A second, independent search (converging on the same Epoché piece plus a
  Barthélémy glossary of Simondon's key terms and a Medium summary of
  Ronald Bogue on Deleuze/Simondon) confirms Simondon's own key mechanism,
  **transduction**, is explicitly generalized across registers: crystal
  genesis (transduction's "simplest image") is treated as one instance of
  a schema Simondon then extends — by "transposition" and "composition,"
  explicitly to avoid reductionism — to biological, psychic, and
  collective individuation. The transindividual itself (per the same NDPR
  review of Combes used in the first verification pass) is defined as "the
  systematic unity of interior (psychic) individuation, and exterior
  (collective) individuation," i.e. built directly on top of this
  cross-register schema rather than being a separate, unrelated theory
  bolted onto physical individuation.
- The NDPR review of Combes, fetched directly for this pass, does *not*
  explicitly address consciousness or co-presence as a dividing line
  between registers — it engages the psychic/collective material more than
  the physical exemplars (noting Simondon's primary physical-individuation
  thesis wasn't fully published in French until 2005, which likely limits
  how much of it English-language secondary literature engages directly).
  This is a real gap: no source located states in so many words "Simondon
  says physical individuation does not require consciousness." What is
  solidly supported instead is the *positive* claim that matters for the
  paper's purposes: crystallization is Simondon's own canonical example of
  individuation, crystals are not conscious and do not co-present with
  anything, and Simondon's own transductive schema explicitly carries that
  same basic operation forward — by structural analogy, not metaphor — into
  the psychic and collective registers.

**Assessment:** The claim the co-author wants ("the runtime event, not
requiring the two human/AI parties to be temporally co-present, still
counts as Simondonian individuation") is a **plausible, well-grounded
extension**, not a category error — Simondon's own physical-register
exemplar already establishes that individuation-in-his-sense does not
inherently require consciousness or presence, and his transductive schema
is explicitly built to generalize across registers rather than treat the
physical case as inapplicable analogy. The paper should still state this
as an extension by structural analogy to Simondon's own cross-register
method, not as something Simondon discusses in relation to computation or
runtime events directly (he obviously doesn't) — same discipline as
Claim 2 above.

## Overall summary

against a primary-adjacent philosophy-encyclopedia source and an
independent political-theory encyclopedia entry, both stating the concept
exactly as the co-author's proposal uses it — no correction needed beyond

made. Claim 2 (agrammaticality) is the one flagged as uncertain going in,
and the honest finding is better than the worry: it is a real term used by
Deleuze & Guattari together, in *A Thousand Plateaus* itself (the
"Postulates of Linguistics" plateau, via the Cummings "he danced his did"

text explicitly calls the atypical/agrammatical expression "a cutting edge
of deterritorialization of language," giving the paper a genuine, existing
D&G-stated link between the two concepts rather than requiring the paper to
manufacture one. What the paper should NOT claim is that agrammaticality is
as central or recurring a concept as deterritorialization itself across the
whole D&G corpus — it is one specific, narrower mechanism (grammar pushed
to and past its own limit) documented in one plateau plus one later
solo-Deleuze essay, not a load-bearing term throughout *Anti-Oedipus* and
*A Thousand Plateaus* the way deterritorialization is. And the specific
move of applying "agrammatic" to a new self-declared predicate expanding
DMML's closed vocabulary is the paper's own synthesis — D&G never discuss
anything resembling a formal, extensible grammar of predicates — and must
be labeled as such. Claim 3 (Simondon, physical-register individuation
without consciousness/co-presence) is ✅ verified as a plausible, genuinely
Simondon-grounded extension: crystallization is Simondon's own canonical
physical-individuation example, and his transductive schema is explicitly
built to generalize across physical/biological/psychic/collective
registers by structural analogy rather than being four unrelated theories —
so "a computational runtime event, not requiring temporal co-presence"
is a defensible extension of Simondon's own method, though no source states
outright that consciousness is unnecessary in those words, so the paper
should phrase this as an inference from the physical exemplar plus the
transductive schema, not as a direct Simondon quotation. All three claims
can proceed into drafting; Claim 2 needs the narrower framing above rather
than being elevated to deterritorialization's own level of centrality, and
both new moves (agrammatic grammar-expansion, runtime-event-as-site) should
be explicitly marked as this paper's own extensions of D&G/Simondon's
concepts rather than presented as claims already made in the primary texts.
