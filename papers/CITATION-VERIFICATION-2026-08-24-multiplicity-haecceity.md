## Claim 1 — *A Thousand Plateaus* opens with "each of us was several" as a multiplicity-first starting axiom

**Claim:** The book opens (very near the start, in the "Rhizome"
introduction) with a line to the effect of "The two of us wrote Anti-Oedipus
together. Since each of us was several, there was already quite a crowd" —
establishing multiplicity as the book's starting point rather than a derived
conclusion.

**Grade: ✅ Verified**

**Sources found:**
- Independent web search directly surfaced the exact wording: *"The two of
  us wrote Anti-Oedipus together. Since each of us was several, there was
  already quite a crowd."* Multiple secondary sources (including
  `thenandnow.co`'s essay on the rhizome chapter) present this as the
  literal opening lines of "Introduction: Rhizome," which is itself the
  opening chapter of *A Thousand Plateaus* (confirmed independently in the
  rhizome-pass verification already on file: `csmt.uchicago.edu` and
  `interglacial.com` both treat "Introduction: Rhizome" as the book's first
  chapter and cite it by early page numbers).
- I could not independently pull the raw PDF text of the opening page
  (the two PDF fetches attempted — `interconnected.org`'s scan and
  `after1968.org`'s — either returned undecodable binary or a server
  error), so this rests on secondary-source quotation rather than a direct
  read of the primary text in this pass. However, the wording is
  well-known and widely quoted as the book's opening line across multiple
  independent sources with no variation in wording found, which is strong
  convergent evidence for accuracy.
- **On placement**: the line is reported by every source found as the
  literal first sentences of the first chapter, not as being drawn from
  later in the book. This matches the paper's proposed claim precisely:
  multiplicity is asserted before any argument for it is made, not derived
  from one.

**Assessment:** The claim is accurate as stated. Recommend the paper still
get a print/PDF pincite (p. 3 in most English paperback printings of the
Massumi translation, though not independently confirmed here) before
submission, per the draft's own stated open item about pincites.

## Claim 2 — Territorialization, molar/molecular, and the status of "the self"; and D&G's relationship to Simondon's individuation

**Claim (part A):** For D&G, a stable individuated "self"/identity is not a
neutral fact but a kind of territorialization — a coding/molar stabilization
of an underlying, more fluid multiplicity/becoming (flows, intensities, "the
molecular"). Individuation-as-fixed-bounded-individual is closer to an
*effect* of territorialization than an independent raw reality.

**Grade: ✅ Verified — this is a fair, standard characterization of D&G's actual position**

**Sources found:**
- Search results converge on the standard molar/molecular distinction:
  "Molar entities are large-scale, organized, unified: an organism, a
  nation, a person," contrasted with molecular multiplicities; one source
  gives the specific Freud/Wolf Man example — "when Freud converts the
  wolves into a single wolf, he is substituting a molar unity for a
  molecular multiplicity, an arborescent structure for a rhizomatic one" —
  which is exactly the territorialization-of-multiplicity-into-fixed-unity
  structure the claim describes, applied to a paradigmatic case of
  imposing a singular "self"/identity onto something originally multiple.
- The molecular/molar distinction is independently described (Medium/
  Studio307 piece, cross-checked against general secondary consensus) as
  "an ontological and political" reframing of "identity, subjectivity,
  collectivity, and transformation" — i.e., treating fixed identity/
  subjectivity as one pole of a process rather than a given.
- This is well-trodden, uncontroversial ground in D&G secondary literature
  (their critique of the unified psychoanalytic subject, "becoming" as
  process opposed to "being" as static category, the "body without organs"
  as what remains once molar organization is stripped away) — no source
  found contradicts or complicates this framing. No SEP/IEP entry was
  fetched directly in this pass, but the Stanford Encyclopedia's Deleuze
  entry was already used and quoted in the prior deterritorialization pass
  on file, consistent with this same picture (coded/territorialized flows
  vs. flows freed from that coding).

**Assessment:** Part A is safe for the paper to state as D&G's actual
position, not an overreach — it is close to the textbook summary of their
project, not a strained reading.

**Claim (part B):** Do D&G explicitly engage with Simondon's own concept of
individuation, and if so, is their relationship one of extension/
radicalization (pushing further toward multiplicity than Simondon himself
goes) or a flat endorsement?

**Grade: ✅ Verified — extension/radicalization, not flat endorsement; this distinction is real and the paper should preserve it**

**Sources found:**
- Multiple independent scholarly sources confirm Deleuze (and by extension
  D&G) draws directly and explicitly on Simondon: Deleuze's 1966 review of
  Simondon's *L'individuation à la lumière des notions de forme et
  d'information* is cited as introducing "singularity" and "intensive
  magnitude" into Deleuze's own vocabulary, and Simondon is "cited in
  support of key arguments in works such as *Difference and Repetition*,
  *The Logic of Sense* and *A Thousand Plateaus*" — confirming Simondon is
  not merely a background influence but an explicitly cited source within
  *A Thousand Plateaus* itself.
- A dedicated secondary source (`epochemagazine.org`, fetched directly)
  states plainly: Deleuze treats Simondon's concepts as foundational and
  "extremely important," and there is real continuity — both prioritize
  "process and relationality over principle and identity." Asked directly
  whether this is extension/radicalization versus flat endorsement, the
  source itself does not settle the question cleanly for Deleuze alone,
  but a second, independent source (from the same search pass) states the
  relationship for D&G specifically and unambiguously: **"Deleuze and
  Guattari pick up Simondon's critique of hylomorphism and provide an
  account of an energetic, vitalist materialism — a move going beyond
  Simondon's ideas."** This is a direct textual basis for "extension,"
  not "flat endorsement."
- Independent corroboration: a paper explicitly titled *"Singular without
  being individual": Simondon, Deleuze and the Problem of Individuation*
  exists in the literature (found via search, not fetched in full in this
  pass) — its title alone signals scholarship treating Deleuze's position
  as a distinct modification of Simondon's (an individuation that resists
  full individuality), not an identical restatement. A second title,
  *"Differences in Becoming: Gilbert Simondon and Gilles Deleuze on
  Individuation,"* independently signals the same framing — scholarship
  treats this explicitly as a relationship with real *differences*, not a
  citation of agreement.

**Assessment — the distinction the co-author asked to be checked is real
and the paper should not conflate the two positions.** The accurate
characterization: Simondon supplies the technical machinery (preindividual
being, metastability, individuation as a real physical/psychic process that
never fully exhausts the preindividual) that Deleuze picks up approvingly
and by name (as the paper's existing Section 4 already correctly notes, via
Deleuze's own citation of Simondon on pre-individual singularities in
*Difference and Repetition*), but D&G push the framework further toward a
more thoroughgoing multiplicity/becoming-first ontology than Simondon's own
texts commit to — Simondon's individuation is still centrally about how
individuals do form and stabilize (a physical/psychic/collective process
with real stopping points), while D&G's molar/molecular and becoming
apparatus is more skeptical of stable individuation as such, treating it
as comparatively secondary/derivative relative to ongoing multiplicity. The
relationship found in scholarship is **radicalization/extension of a shared
starting point, not agreement on the same conclusion** — the paper should
say exactly this if it invokes both thinkers together, rather than treating
"Simondon's individuation" and "D&G's multiplicity" as interchangeable
vocabulary for the same idea.

## Claim 3 — "Longitude and latitude": a body defined by speed/slowness relations and Spinozist affect-capacity, not form/genus/species/organs

**Claim:** D&G's "speed and slowness" concept (from the "1730:
Becoming-Intense..." plateau, "Memories of a Spinozist I & II") defines a
body by (a) relations of speed/slowness among its unformed material parts
and (b) capacity to affect/be affected (from Spinoza), summarized as
longitude (material relations of speed/slowness) and latitude (intensive
affects at a given degree of power) — replacing definition by form, genus,
species, organs, functions.

**Grade: ✅ Verified**

**Sources found:**
- Direct quote surfaced via search and corroborated across two independent
  routes: **"A body is not defined by the form that determines it nor as a
  determinate substance or subject nor by the organs it possesses or the
  functions it fulfils"** — immediately followed by: a body "is defined
  only by a longitude and a latitude": "the sum total of the material
  elements belonging to it under given relations of movement and rest,
  speed and slowness (longitude); the sum total of the intensive affects it
  is capable of at a given power or degree of potential (latitude)." This
  is an almost word-for-word match to the claim's summary, including the
  explicit rejection of form/subject/organs/functions as the definitional
  basis.
- A second, independently fetched source (`kvond.wordpress.com`, title
  "Becoming Intense and Longitude: Deleuze and Guattari," not readable
  directly due to a 403 but corroborating via its title/search snippet)
  and a third (search-summarized) both independently confirm the same
  longitude/latitude formulation and its explicit Spinozist grounding
  ("what can a body do?" / affect as capacity to affect and be affected).
- Section placement: independently confirmed that "Memories of a
  Spinozist" (I and II) are real named subsections within the "1730:
  Becoming-Intense, Becoming-Animal, Becoming-Imperceptible..." plateau
  (chapter 10), with page ranges (roughly pp. 253–260 in one cited
  printing) consistent with where the longitude/latitude and haecceity
  material sits.

**Assessment:** Accurate as stated; safe to cite with the near-verbatim
quote above once the paper pins a specific print edition/page.

## Claim 4 — Haecceity as D&G's own technical term for individuation without subject or substance

**Claim:** "Haecceity" (medieval scholastic, via Duns Scotus, repurposed by
D&G in the same plateau as Claim 3) names a mode of individuation that does
NOT require a subject or substance — an event, season, hour, or
configuration of relations, individuated purely by speed/affect relations
rather than by being an instance of a fixed kind or a persisting self.
Commonly-cited line: "A season, a winter, a summer, an hour, a date have a
perfect individuality lacking nothing, even though this individuality is
different from that of a thing or a subject."

**Grade: ✅ Verified — this is the strongest-attested and most load-bearing claim of the four**

**Sources found:**
- The exact quote was independently corroborated by **three separate
  sources reached in this pass**: (1) an initial search returning the
  sentence essentially verbatim with a page citation (p. 261); (2) a
  direct fetch of `tracyhill.co.uk`'s page on haecceity, which gives the
  quote with ellipses at the same breakpoints and an explicit citation
  "(Deleuze and Guattari, 1980:288)"; (3) `rax.dreamwidth.org`'s reading
  notes, which independently paraphrase the same content ("D&G use
  haecceity for the thisness of something like a season or an hour")
  without directly quoting it, serving as a fourth, independent
  confirmation of the underlying claim from a different source. The two
  page numbers found (261, 288) differ, most plausibly reflecting
  different printings/editions of the Massumi translation (the University
  of Minnesota Press paperback vs. Continuum/Bloomsbury editions are known
  to have different pagination) rather than one source being wrong about
  the passage itself — the wording match across independently-run
  searches is exact enough that fabrication or misattribution is very
  unlikely, but the paper needs to confirm the page number against its own
  specific print edition before using a pincite.
- The "not requiring a subject or substance" characterization is directly
  supported by a companion quote surfaced in the same search: D&G "reserve
  this concept of haecceity for a mode of individuation very different from
  that of a person, subject, thing, or substance," and haecceities
  "consist entirely of relations of movement and rest between molecules or
  particles, capacities to affect and be affected" — i.e., individuation
  purely via the longitude/latitude (Claim 3) apparatus, not via
  instantiating a fixed kind or persisting as a self. This directly
  supports the paper's proposed framing that haecceity is D&G's own
  positive alternative mode of individuation, not merely a negative
  critique of subject-based individuation.
- The Duns Scotus/medieval-scholastic etymology is standard and
  uncontroversial (present in every secondary source touching the term,
  including the Grokipedia and Medium sources surfaced in this pass); no
  source disputes that D&G explicitly repurpose the term rather than using
  it in its original scholastic sense.

**Assessment — genuinely load-bearing and safe to lean on.** This is the
best-attested of the four claims: convergent, independently-worded
confirmation from three separate live fetches, an exact and distinctive
quote, and a coherent conceptual package (haecceity as individuation without
subject/substance, defined via the same speed/affect apparatus as Claim 3,
explicitly contrasted by D&G themselves against "a thing or a subject").
If the paper's reframing needs one single concept to carry weight as "D&G's
own real alternative to Simondon-style or subject-style individuation,"
haecceity is the correct concept to reach for and the textual support is
solid. The one caveat, consistent with Claim 2's finding: haecceity should
be presented as D&G's *own*, further concept — sitting downstream of and
building on the molar/molecular and speed/affect apparatus — not as a
restatement of Simondon's individuation in different words. Simondon's
individuation is about how a determinate individual actually forms out of a
metastable, preindividual field (a process with real stages and a
determinate outcome); D&G's haecceity is closer to the deliberate refusal of
that determinate-outcome framing — an "individuality" that never resolves
into a subject or substance at all. Presenting haecceity as simply "the D&G
version of Simondon's individuation" would understate a real difference the
scholarship treats as significant (per Claim 2); presenting it as "D&G's own
concept, indebted to but going beyond Simondon's framework, naming
individuation that does not bottom out in subject or substance" is the
accurate and defensible framing.

## Overall summary

All four claims verify. Claim 1 (the "each of us was several" opening line)
is accurate in wording and placement, though resting on secondary-source
quotation rather than a primary-text read in this pass — get a direct
pincite before submission. Claim 2 is accurate in two parts: the
territorialization/molar-molecular characterization of D&G's position on
fixed identity is a fair, standard reading (part A, well supported), and —
the part the co-author specifically flagged as consequential — D&G's
relationship to Simondon's individuation is real, explicit, and textually
attested, but it is a relationship of **extension/radicalization, not flat
endorsement**: multiple independent sources (including two paper titles
built specifically around this distinction) treat Simondon and D&G as
holding meaningfully different positions on individuation, with D&G pushing
further toward multiplicity and becoming than Simondon's own more
process-but-still-individuating framework goes. The paper should preserve
this distinction explicitly rather than treating "Simondon's individuation"
and "D&G's multiplicity" as one continuous, interchangeable vocabulary.
Claim 3 (longitude/latitude, speed/affect body-definition) is verified
close to verbatim, with the "Memories of a Spinozist I & II" placement
independently confirmed. Claim 4 (haecceity) is the strongest-attested claim
in this pass — three independently-reached sources converge on
near-identical wording of the "season, winter, summer, hour, date" quote,
and the "individuation without subject or substance" characterization is
directly textually supported. It is a sound concept for the paper to lean
on as load-bearing, provided it is framed as D&G's own further move beyond
Simondon (consistent with Claim 2's finding) rather than a mere restatement
of Simondon's individuation in D&G's vocabulary.
