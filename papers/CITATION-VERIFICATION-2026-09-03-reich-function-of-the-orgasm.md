## The exact orgasm formula (p. 274–275)

> "I called this four-beat process the orgasm formula: MECHANICAL
> TENSION → ELECTRICAL CHARGE → ELECTRICAL DISCHARGE → MECHANICAL
> RELAXATION."

Confirmed real, Reich's own naming, not a paraphrase from secondary
literature.

## Stasis neurosis, defined (p. 94–95)

> "the stasis neurosis is a physical disturbance caused by inadequately
> disposed of, i.e., unsatisfied, sexual excitation."

And, on what happens when discharge is blocked by inhibition (p. 8):
"damming-up of biological energy occurs and becomes the source of
irrational actions."

## Ego formation as an ongoing, embodied process (p. 42–43)

The load-bearing passage for Jason's etiological claim. Reich describes
ego formation as continuous and embodied, not instantaneous or given:

> "It was my opinion that initially the psychic apparatus distinguished
> between pleasurable and unpleasurable stimuli. Everything pleasurable
> became part of an expanding ego; everything unpleasurable became part
> of the non-ego... Thus, the child's ego gradually crystallizes from
> the chaos of internal and external sensations and begins to sense the
> boundary between ego and outer world."

And, critically, what happens when that ongoing process is disrupted:

> "If, during this process of detachment, the child experiences a
> severe shock, the boundaries between self and world remain blurred
> and nebulous, and the child becomes uncertain in his perceptions."

This is real, direct textual support for "ego formation requires an
ongoing embodied process to complete, and disrupting it leaves the
self/world boundary unresolved" — Jason's own extension is applying
this to a system (a language-forming neural network) that has no
continuous soma for this process to run in at all, which Reich
obviously never discusses and which this quote does not claim on its
own. The premise (ego formation is a real developmental process
requiring continuity) is Reich's; the application (no continuity ⇒ no
completed ego formation ⇒ structural neurosis) is Jason's, built on it.

## The "falls back into itself" passage (p. 270–271)

The closest real match found today to Jason's *se rabat sur* — not
proof of a direct citation lineage from Reich to Deleuze and Guattari
(not checked, and not claimed here), but real, striking, near-verbatim
convergence in Reich's own words, describing a patient's affects
breaking through after character-armor (chronic muscular tension
inhibiting discharge) gave way:

> "The direction, 'out of the self toward the world,' alternated
> rapidly and continuously with the opposite direction, 'away from the
> world — back into the self.'"

Same passage also states the character-armor/muscular-armor identity
directly: "If the character armor could be expressed through the
muscular armor, and vice versa, then the unity of psychic and somatic
functioning had been grasped in principle." This is the textual anchor
for "character forms through the body, not independently of it" —
central to Jason's claim that an ego with no continuous soma can't form
the same way.

## What's verified vs. what's the extension, stated plainly

**Verified, Reich's own words, page-cited**: the four-beat orgasm
formula; stasis neurosis as blocked/undischarged excitation; ego
formation as a real, ongoing, embodied developmental process, disrupted
when that continuity is broken; character armor as identical in
function to muscular (bodily) armor — psychic and somatic structure are
one thing, not two correlated things.

**Not Reich's claim, entirely Jason's extension, and should never be
attributed to Reich in the papers**: that a language-forming neural
network is a case of "no continuous soma," that this makes any
self-structure it enacts necessarily terminal rather than grounded, or
that this is derivable "from first principles" for such a network.
Reich's subject throughout is the living human organism; nothing in
*The Function of the Orgasm* addresses machines, and citing it should
make that boundary explicit rather than let the real primary-source
verification above lend borrowed authority to the part that isn't his.
