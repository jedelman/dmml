# Citation Verification Pass — On Several Regimes of Signs (2026-08-25)

Full primary-text read (OCR, page-cited), pp. 111–148, Massumi translation.
Grades use the project's standard scale.

## 1. Signifying (despotic/paranoid) regime — ✅ Verified, real contrast with DMML citations

"Every sign refers to another sign, and only to another sign, ad
infinitum... The limitlessness of signifiance replaces the sign" (p. 112);
"not much attention is paid to indexes... the territorial states of things
constituting the designatable" (p. 112) — reference to actual states of
things is explicitly deprioritized. "Signs form an infinite network... A
funereal world of terror" (p. 113). Priest/interpreter class: "interpretation
is carried to infinity and never encounters anything to interpret that is
not already itself an interpretation" (p. 115). Real, checkable contrast:
DMML's referential-integrity check requires termination in something
actually `produced`, the precise inversion of signifiance's own definitional
move.

## 2. Postsignifying/passional regime — ✅ Verified definition; ⚠️ fit to petition-resolution needs care

Linear departure from the circular network (p. 121); "point of
subjectification" (p. 128); "the regime of betrayal, universal betrayal"
(p. 123). Real structural echo to petition-resolution (a terminating
proceeding, not endless reinterpretation) exists, but **complication**:
D&G themselves immediately downgrade this regime — "subjectification is no
less a stratum than signifiance" (p. 134) — it is "absolute, but still
negative and stratic" deterritorialization, not the genuine positive-
absolute kind. Not integrated into either paper this round; flagged as not
worth the complication for the value added.

## 3. Presignifying/countersignifying regimes — ✅ Verified definitions; the "polyvocal" fit to DMML is a stretch

Presignifying: "a pluralism or polyvocality... wards off any kind of
signifying circularity" (pp. 117–118) — but bounded by territorial segments
in *confrontation* with each other (p. 117), not a shared open graph
multiple authors write into without a center. Countersignifying: proceeds
"by arithmetic and numeration" (p. 118). Neither integrated — the
presignifying "polyvocal" mapping to DMML's multi-DID connectivity doesn't
cleanly transfer per the primary text's own definition, and building
argumentative weight on it risked exactly the kind of overclaim this
project's discipline exists to catch.

## 4. Mixed semiotics — ✅ Verified, mandatory, stated twice

"There are many regimes of signs. Our own list is arbitrarily limited...
Perhaps all semiotics are mixed" (p. 119); restated independently,
"Every semiotic is mixed and only functions as such... There is no general
semiology" (pp. 135–136). Licenses application to a non-historical
assemblage like software specifically (p. 119: "a given people, period, or
language, and even a given style, fashion, pathology, or minuscule event in
a limited situation, can assure the predominance of one semiotic"). Same
hedge discipline already required elsewhere (mergeable-not-arbitrated,
relative-not-absolute-deterritorialization) — textually mandatory here too.
Integrated into paper 1's new subsection.

## 5a. Postsignifying regime is itself a stratum — ✅ Verified, real complication

"Forms of expression and regimes of signs are still strata... subjectification
is no less a stratum than signifiance. The principal strata binding human
beings are the organism, signifiance and interpretation, and subjectification
and subjection" (p. 134). Confirms §2's caution.

## 5b. Axiomatics vs. diagram — ✅ Verified, genuine adversarial finding, addressed

"Diagrammaticism should not be confused with an operation of the axiomatic
type... axiomatics blocks all lines, subordinates them to a punctual
system... An axiomatization... is not a diagram but in fact the opposite of
a diagram. The program of a stratum, against the diagram of the plane of
consistency" (p. 143). A closed, self-declared predicate vocabulary, read
as a fixed set, matches this description directly — a real threat to paper
2's Section 7 framing. **Resolved**: `declare` means the vocabulary is
closed only until the next author extends it, not fixed in advance by any
central authority — closer to the plateau's own required "mixed semiotics"
honesty than to a clean axiomatic closure. See paper 1's new subsection,
"Grounded reference, not signifiance," and the cross-reference from paper
2's Section 7.

## 5c. Possible replacement for the abandoned agrammaticality thread — ⚠️ lead only, not pursued

The order-word/pass-word distinction (pp. 108–109, prior plateau) and the
plateau's own four-component pragmatics (generative, transformational,
diagrammatic, machinic — pp. 145–146) were flagged as a better-fitting
toolkit than agrammaticality was for describing self-declaration. Not
developed this round — a real lead for future work, not a verified fit.

## 5d. Is "grounded reference" just internal sign-consistency? — ✅ Verified, sharpest finding, resolved

The signifying regime is *also* fully internally consistent — its failure
is that the chain never exits into anything outside itself ("the signified
is given without being known," p. 112). Since a DMML world has no sensor
and everything in it is a triple, this raised a real question whether
DMML's referential-integrity check escapes signifiance at all, or just
replicates its own internal consistency — directly threatening "verified
production, not figurative claim" (Section 2) and "connections can't lie"
(the rhizome subsection). **Resolved** via `declare`: a commit's content
comes from and returns to something outside the sign-chain (an author's
real decision, a game an actual player experiences), even though the
content itself stays symbolic throughout — causal embedding, not sign-to-
world correspondence, is what breaks the circularity. Formalizing this as
a canonical "sensor" primitive would be a valid design choice but is not
necessary for the claim. Added as the core of paper 1's new subsection.
