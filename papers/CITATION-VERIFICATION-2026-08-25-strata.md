# Citation Verification Pass — Strata / Deterritorialization Apparatus (2026-08-25)

Follow-on to the 2026-08-24 passes. Prompted by feedback that Section 4 was
leaning too heavily on Simondon's individuation and not enough on *A Thousand
Plateaus*' own apparatus — differentiation and deterritorialization/
reterritorialization on, against, and off the plane of consistency through
various strata — which is the actual phenomenon the paper is reaching for.
Grades use the project's standard scale: ✅ Verified · ⚠️ Plausible, unverified
· ❌ Wrong · 🚫 Unverifiable · ☠️ Fabricated.

**Method note:** claims 1–3 verified against a direct read of the Massumi
translation of "10,000 B.C.: The Geology of Morals" (*A Thousand Plateaus*,
University of Minnesota Press, 1987, pp. 39–74) and the "Rhizome"
introduction (pp. 3–25), not secondary paraphrase. Claims 4–5 are
secondary/tertiary-sourced and graded accordingly.

## 1. Strata as double articulation — ✅ Verified

Direct quotes, pp. 40–43: strata are "acts of capture," operating "by coding
and territorialization"; double articulation is a first articulation
(sedimentation — selecting metastable units from unstable flows, imposing a
statistical order) and a second (folding — establishing stable functional
structure). Content/expression is explicitly Hjelmslev's vocabulary, adopted
and stripped of linguistic specificity by D&G ("if language has a specificity
of its own... that specificity consists neither in double articulation nor in
Hjelmslev's net, which are general characteristics of strata," p. 43) — the
paper should credit Hjelmslev if it uses content/expression language, not
present it as D&G's own coinage. The plane of consistency/BwO is what strata
capture flows *from*: "permeated by unformed, unstable matters, by flows in
all directions... the body without organs is itself the plane of consistency,
which becomes compact... at the level of the strata" (p. 40).

## 2. Deterritorialization/reterritorialization as a coupled pair; absolute vs. relative — ✅ Verified

"Deterritorialization... is always relative, and has reterritorialization as
its flipside or complement" (p. 54). Absolute deterritorialization is
explicitly distinguished and tied to the plane of consistency itself: "The
former are stratic or interstratic, whereas the latter concern the plane of
consistency and its destratification" (p. 55); D&G treat absolute
deterritorialization as ontologically primary, not a rare escape from a
default relative state: "It is the strata that are always residue, not the
opposite" (p. 56). Important, citable nuance: the relative/absolute
distinction is explicitly NOT about speed — "What qualifies a
deterritorialization is not its speed... but its nature" (p. 56), whether it
proceeds by articulated, segmentary steps (relative) or "jumps from one
singularity to another following a nondecomposable, nonsegmentary line"
(absolute).

## 3. Plane of consistency vs. strata/Ecumenon — ✅ Verified, one correction

"The plane of consistency knows nothing of differences in level... nothing of
the distinction between contents and expressions" (p. 69); what moves on it
once destratified is explicitly heterogeneous and non-hierarchical ("a
semiotic fragment rubs shoulders with a chemical interaction... The plane of
consistency is the abolition of all metaphor; all that consists is Real,"
p. 69). **Correction**: the pairing this chapter itself uses is *plane of
consistency (Planomenon) vs. strata/Ecumenon* ("the Ecumenon, as opposed to
the Planomenon of the plane of consistency," p. 49), not "plane of
organization" — that term belongs to Plateau 10 ("Becoming-Intense"), not
Plateau 3. Rhizome's connection/heterogeneity principle as what operates on
the plane of consistency is a well-grounded synthetic reading, not a sentence
D&G write explicitly — flag it as the paper's own connective move.

## 4. Differentiation vs. individuation as D&G's preferred register — ⚠️ Plausible, unverified as a stated scholarly claim

No single source states this exact comparative claim outright. Secondary
literature (Voss's "The Problem of Method: Deleuze and Simondon" and
"Modulating Matters: Simondon, Deleuze and Guattari," full text not
retrievable) converges on D&G "going beyond Simondon's ideas," not restating
them. The strongest available support is the primary text itself (claims
1–3): D&G's own apparatus has no stable individual as an end-state — strata,
deterritorialization, and the plane of consistency describe an ongoing
capture/release process with no terminus, structurally different from
Simondon's individuation converging on a real, locatable individual. The
paper should present this as its own synthesis argued from the primary text,
not attribute it to existing scholarship it can't currently cite directly.
