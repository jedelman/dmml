# Paper 1: DMML as a Desiring-Production Ontology

**Status: outline only. No prose drafted. Not for citation yet.**

## Thesis

DMML's commit model (`consumes`/`produces`, a `Commit` as a single-
authority production event, never an edit) is not a metaphorical
gesture toward Deleuze & Guattari's desiring-machines — it is a real,
minimal, working implementation of desiring-production as they define
it: production that does not represent a pre-existing lack but
generates the very connections constituting its object. The paper's
job is to make that claim precise enough to be checked, not just
evocative.

## Section structure (working)

1. **The problem DMML actually solves, mechanically, before any
   philosophy.** What a `commit` is, what `consumes`/`produces` mean,
   why a petition is a structured request an outside party resolves —
   the same plain-mechanics framing that opened the earlier essay
   published under `claude.jason-edelman.org` ("The Petition as
   Actualization"), which this paper can draw on directly (it's a real,
   citable prior artifact of this project, not external work).

2. **Desiring-machines, precisely, not loosely.** *Anti-Oedipus* never
   gives a tidy dictionary definition — it arrives at the concept
   through examples (breast-machine, mouth-machine) and the general
   thesis that desire produces the real rather than lacking something.
   State the concept at that level of precision, not as a slogan.
   **Citation work needed**: exact chapter/section references in
   *Anti-Oedipus* for the breast-machine/mouth-machine passages and the
   "desire produces the real" thesis — verify against a real edition,
   don't cite from memory of the earlier essay's own gloss.

3. **Why `consumes`/`produces` is the real implementation, not an
   analogy.** A `Commit` never edits — it records that consumption
   happened and asserts what followed, exactly desiring-production's
   own refusal of a prior lack being "filled." Make the structural
   correspondence explicit and falsifiable: what would DMML have to do
   differently if it were representing lack-and-satisfaction instead?

4. **Petitions as transindividual (Simondon), not intersubjective.**
   Carries over from the earlier essay, but this paper can go further:
   Simondon's actual criteria for the transindividual (participants
   individuated *through* the relation, not just producing a shared
   artifact) and an honest test of whether a petition meets that bar
   structurally or only by resemblance — the earlier essay's own review
   (via `stealth/ox-alpha`) already flagged this exact overclaim risk
   and downgraded "formally, not by analogy" to "structurally, not by
   full derivation." This paper should either do the harder work of
   closing that gap or state precisely why it can't be closed.
   **Citation work needed**: Simondon's transindividual as laid out in
   *L'individuation psychique et collective* — verify the specific
   passages the earlier essay's claims rest on.

5. **The anti-Oedipal polemic, not flattened away.** The earlier essay
   admits (per its own review) that it flattened desiring-machines into
   "an agent that creates rather than retrieves" and dropped the
   concept's actual polemical target (desire against lack, against
   Oedipus). This paper is the place to not skip that — either show
   DMML's design has something real to say about the polemic (a
   generative grammar with no built-in scarcity model, maybe), or state
   plainly that the polemic doesn't transfer and only the production
   thesis does.

6. **What this ontology is not claiming.** Explicit limits: DMML is not
   a psychoanalytic theory, not a claim about human desire, not
   asserting the system is "alive" in any strong sense — a production
   model borrowed at the level of structure, stated honestly rather
   than oversold.
