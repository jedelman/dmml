# DMML as a Desiring-Production Ontology

*Philosophy of technology / STS framing. Exact pincites for the Deleuze/Guattari and Simondon material are marked pending below and are needed before submission; the drafting history behind this text lives in this repository's own commit log and `dev-journal/`, not in the paper.*

## Abstract

DMML is a small declarative language for text-adventure world models: a `commit` block asserts what a world's state consumed and what it produced, and nothing else is a primitive. This paper argues DMML's commit model is a real, checkable structural correspondence to Deleuze and Guattari's desiring-production, not a decorative metaphor, and is equally careful about where the correspondence runs out. Its connectivity is rhizomatic, not arborescent, in Deleuze and Guattari's own specified sense — a claim that survives comparison to git, blockchain, the open web, and ordinary linked data, via a further, narrowly-stated property: DMML's edges cannot dangle unnoticed — grounding, not a guarantee of semantic fidelity. DMML's own grammar is better read through Deleuze and Guattari's apparatus of strata, double articulation, and deterritorialization/reterritorialization on and off a plane of consistency than through Simondon's individuation, which converges on a stable individual DMML's own multiplicity keeps deferring; a small, runnable simulation (a "pantheon" of independently-authored commits) grounds every claim below. On this reading DMML is not merely rhizomatic but auto-recombinant: its own accumulated multiplicity is a first-class input to further production, an ordinary act of the grammar. Charge inheres in the fact of change itself — what Deleuze and Guattari call speed. We indicate the direction this points; we do not claim to have proven it arrives.

## 1. The mechanism, before any philosophy

A DMML world is a directed history of `commit` blocks. Each commit declares what it `consumes` — references to prior facts or nodes it depends on — and what it `produces` — new triples asserted into the world's graph. There is no primitive for editing a fact in place: an attribute change is itself a commit that consumes the old value and produces a new one, and the old commit's data stays physically in the history, only superseded for the purpose of reading current state. Retraction is the same shape with an empty `produces`.

Two distinct things are true here and should not be run together. First, at the level of the portable reference implementation itself: a `consumes` entry that cites a fact nothing actually produced is a no-op, not an error — the interpreter's own retraction step simply finds nothing to retract and moves on, a deliberate, formally-specified design decision (`fact_retraction_fails_open`), not an oversight. A commit that cites something fabricated is still accepted; it just fails to retract what it claimed to. Second, and separately: whether a commit gets *admitted into a world's history at all* is a substrate-specific question this portable layer does not answer on its own — written-world's atproto-backed deployment gates admission on a real, live compare-and-swap against its repository (issue #53), which is where anything resembling "was this citation genuine at write time" actually gets enforced, if it does. What both layers agree on, and what genuinely holds regardless of which is in view: neither one verifies that a reference is *current* (the most recent state of whatever it points to), nor that a new commit's `produces` is *semantically consistent* with what it consumes beyond naming something real. An older reference is accepted, not rejected as stale. Two commits can cite the same real prior fact and produce contradictory claims about what follows from it, and nothing at either layer stops either one. Citation in DMML is checkable, not policed — by a reader who goes and looks, manually or with tooling this paper does not claim exists, not by the grammar itself. A fabricated or stale reference is not silently laundered into truth for that reader; keeping it out of the log in the first place is substrate work, not a property of the grammar.

A **petition** is a structured request raised against something underdetermined — a player asks what is behind a locked door, and no commit yet answers it — which an outside party (in practice, an AI acting as an author, operating under a persistent, signing identity rather than as a stateless one-off process) resolves. The resolution is itself a commit: it consumes the petition and produces the new fact, permanently linked back to the request. Nothing in DMML's grammar or interpreter computes an expected answer from the request; the request only establishes that some production is now outstanding, not what its content will be.

Concretely, one lifecycle: (1) a player commit `produces` a `Petition` node asking what is behind `door/1`; (2) an author commit `consumes` that petition and `produces` a new room, `room/9`, plus a fact linking `door/1` to it; (3) a later, unrelated commit — the player unlocking `door/1` — `consumes` the door's prior state and cites the room-linking fact from (2) as part of what it builds on. Step (3) is checked only for whether the fact it cites was genuinely produced in (2), not for whether (2)'s content was itself the "right" answer to (1)'s question, and not against whether some other commit has since produced a conflicting claim about `door/1`. That last gap is real and is what Section 4 turns on.

## 2. Desiring-machines, precisely

*Anti-Oedipus* opens through examples before definition: "Everywhere it is machines — real ones, not figurative ones." The breast produces milk; the mouth, coupled to it, is immediately multifunctional.<sup>[pincite pending, ch. 1 §1, "The Desiring-Machines"]</sup> Desire is not lack seeking satisfaction; desire *produces*, and what it produces is real.<sup>[same]</sup>

"Real ones, not figurative ones" is aimed at desire's own self-understanding, not a claim that any literal software instantiates the thesis. DMML's primitives can be checked against the production-not-lack thesis as a structural constraint. Section 3 does that check.

## 3. What the check actually shows, and what it doesn't

A system built on lack-and-satisfaction would need some representation of a deficit that a later event fills — a slot whose shape is fixed in advance, with the later event's job being to match it. DMML's grammar has no such primitive at the level of the language itself: nothing in the interpreter computes an expected or required `produces` from a `consumes`. That is a real, checkable fact about the grammar.

It is also narrower than "DMML is free of lack." Lack does not require a `LACK` predicate to be operative — it can live entirely in *content*: a locked door, a quest structure, a goal state a player is working toward, are all lack-shaped regardless of what the underlying grammar's primitives are. A world authored in DMML could be saturated with deficit-and-completion structure at the content layer while the grammar underneath stays clean of any built-in lack primitive. The claim that actually survives: **DMML's grammar does not impose lack-and-satisfaction as its own operative logic; it does not prevent, and has no opinion about, authors choosing to write lack-shaped content on top of it.** A claim about what the ontology requires, not about what every possible world built on it will do.

A second complication: the petition's resolution is, in this project's current implementation, produced by a large language model sampling from a learned distribution over its training data — reasonably describable as selection from an extremely large menu of already-determined possibilities, close to the very thing this section distinguishes DMML from. Whether sampling from a learned distribution counts as production in Deleuze and Guattari's sense is a separate, genuinely open question about generative models generally, not something DMML's own design settles. Whatever the resolver's own internal process is, DMML's *grammar and interpreter* do not themselves compute a canonical target the resolver is checked against. The system-level absence of a built-in canonical-answer function is the claim; what happens inside whatever black box supplies the answer is out of scope.

The claim above should be read as checkable, not falsifiable in a stronger sense — the only actual falsifying event would be changing the software itself, a design commitment stated in conditional form, not an empirical hypothesis exposed to independent test. This is a claim about what the current grammar requires, offered so a reader can check it against the actual interpreter code rather than take it on the paper's word.

An earlier version of this project's own AI-driven content generation computed petition-like answers from a fixed content model, and produced content that felt derivative — "content that just re-asserts something already true." The fix, arrived at from game-design concerns before any philosophical framing was applied to it, removed exactly the canonical-answer computation this section argues against. The diagnosis converges with Deleuze and Guattari's own critique of representational, selection-based models of desire: a small, independently-arrived-at instance of a critique they share with a broader anti-representational tradition, discovered by engineering failure rather than derived from theory.

## 4. Multiplicity, strata, and the auto-recombinant rhizome

Simondon's transindividual (relation as site of individuation, not a channel between pre-formed individuals)<sup>[pincite pending; cf. Combes, *Gilbert Simondon and the Philosophy of the Transindividual*]</sup> still converges on a determinate outcome, which a petition's resolution doesn't. Deleuze and Guattari's own apparatus — strata, double articulation, deterritorialization/reterritorialization, the plane of consistency — is this section's register instead, developed independently of Simondon. `dmml/examples/pantheon.rs` grounds every structural claim below against real interpreter output, not prose assertion.

### The connectivity is rhizomatic, not arborescent

"Introduction: Rhizome" opens with multiplicity as a starting condition: "The two of us wrote *Anti-Oedipus* together. Since each of us was several, there was already quite a crowd."<sup>[content verified via convergent secondary sourcing; print pincite pending]</sup> Four of six named principles are directly relevant: **connection and heterogeneity**, **multiplicity**, **asignifying rupture** ("a rhizome may be broken, shattered at a given spot, but it will start up again on one of its old lines"), and **cartography over tracing** ("an antigenealogy").<sup>[asignifying rupture and antigenealogy verified 2026-08-25; print pincite pending]</sup> Asignifying rupture names, in the text's own words, the core dynamic below: a rupture doesn't delete the old lines, it starts up again alongside them. "1440: The Smooth and the Striated" gives connection a positive spatial register — **smooth space** as Riemannian patchwork ("pieces that can be joined together in an infinite number of ways," "no top or bottom") against **striated space**, never a clean binary, only mixture — with D&G's own hedge against romanticizing it: "Never believe that a smooth space will suffice to save us."<sup>[verified 2026-08-25, pp. 474–477, 500]</sup> Where rhizome/tree states DMML's connectivity negatively (not arborescent), patchwork states it positively: pieces joinable in many ways, no top and no bottom, exactly `pantheon.rs`'s shape.

Git and blockchain are not the right comparisons, and not equivalent to each other. Blockchain is fairly arborescent: Nakamoto consensus collapses competing forks to one canonical chain, a unity rhizome multiplicity refuses. Git is closer but still tree-like only by convention (the "main" branch), not structure — its DAG allows multi-parent merges and forced convergence is nowhere required. DMML's connectivity reduces to neither, for a plainer reason: **nothing in the grammar or reference interpreter enforces convergence on a `(subject, predicate)` pair at all** — no declared "one winner" policy exists to opt into. `dmml/examples/pantheon.rs` demonstrates this directly: three DID-authored commits (Helios, Selene, Eos) assert different origins for the same node, none referencing any other, and the materializer accepts all three without complaint. A `consumes` entry can cite multiple prior facts at once; `FactRef` addressing cites one specific triple within any prior commit, not only "the tip"; and none of this is bounded to one repository — a foreign-DID quotation cites a node from an entirely separate repository as a first-person percept, a connection git's object graph has no lightweight native mechanism for (a submodule pins a SHA reference, not a copy, but still needs a separate fetch/checkout step; a `FactRef` resolves the same way any other citation does).

The hyperlinked web, the Semantic Web's RDF/linked-data model, and an academic citation network all satisfy connection/heterogeneity, multiplicity, and cross-boundary linking about as well as DMML does — rhizomatic topology alone can't be the whole argument, or the open web would already qualify. What distinguishes DMML: the *commit* carrying a fabricated citation is accepted regardless, at the portable-crate level (Section 1) — but the citation itself does nothing, silently, so a reader who checks a specific citation against the log finds the gap rather than a laundered fact. This is weaker than "connections can't lie." `pantheon.rs` demonstrates the rest directly: because nothing forces a single winner, a rival claim persists as one more citable, disputable production rather than getting silently promoted to canonical status the way an uncontested hyperlink can. The council commit adds a further, distinct move: `canonicalOrigin` is declared on top of the still-divergent `origin` history as an ordinary, separate, revisable fact — not a rewrite, and not erasing Helios's, Selene's, or Eos's own commits. Disputes here are structurally cheap to keep present, not costly to preserve against a system trying to converge them — asignifying rupture almost verbatim: a fork doesn't delete the lines it forked from.

### Strata, capture, and the plane of consistency

A stratum is never freestanding or singular for D&G: "Each stratum serves as the substratum for another stratum," no fixed order — "there is no lesser, no higher or lower, organization; the substratum is an integral part of the stratum."<sup>[pp. 64, 73]</sup> A single stratum also decomposes internally into **epistrata** and **parastrata**, "themselves... strata."<sup>[pp. 50–52]</sup> Nothing below claims DMML, or any part of it, "is a stratum" singularly. A stratum is defined by **double articulation** — first articulation selects a statistical order from unstable flows, second folds that selection into stable structure<sup>[pp. 40–41]</sup> — worked geologically (sedimentation, then folding); the content/expression vocabulary naming the two is Hjelmslev's, not D&G's own coinage.<sup>[p. 43]</sup> What strata capture flows *from* is the **plane of consistency**/body without organs, prior to capture holding together without pre-given hierarchy: "the abolition of all metaphor; all that consists is Real."<sup>[p. 69; the chapter's own contrastive pairing is plane of consistency/Planomenon vs. strata/Ecumenon, not "plane of organization," which belongs elsewhere]</sup> Capture is never final: deterritorialization and reterritorialization are a coupled pair.<sup>[p. 54]</sup> "Capture" also names a separate, later apparatus — "7000 B.C.: Apparatus of Capture" tracks archaic overcoding and capitalist flow-tapping, both extractive, capture defined as "this difference or excess constitutive of profit, surplus labor, or the surplus product," rent/profit/tax its three forms.<sup>[pp. 442, 446–447, 451]</sup> The stratic sense used here is marked off by that feature's absence from its own definition — extraction is the defining mark of the apparatus sense, absent from the stratic one, and usage here stays on that side throughout.

Helios's, Selene's, and Eos's independent commits — three uncoordinated DID-authored claims about `sky/1`'s origin — sit on something structurally like the plane of consistency (Check 1–2 above). Nyx's commit performs double articulation directly: `consumes` selects specific prior facts out of the wider, uncoordinated flow; `produces` folds that selection into one new, stable triple (`duskweave`) that existed in none of the three inputs. An ordinary SQL `JOIN`, git merge, or SPARQL `CONSTRUCT` does the same bare shape — no surprise, since D&G's own double articulation is explicitly general, not linguistic, and worked geologically, not in software.<sup>[p. 43]</sup> What distinguishes DMML's instance: the facts Nyx folds together are, unlike an arbitrary SQL row, grounded against a real production history (Section 1); unlike a git merge's parents, they can originate from separate sovereign repositories with no shared authority; unlike a SPARQL `CONSTRUCT` over an unverified graph, nothing required the claims to be reconciled or deduplicated first. That combination — double articulation through grounded, sovereignty-crossing, non-convergent connectivity — is what this paper calls **auto-recombinant**: accumulated multiplicity as a first-class input to further production, `consumes`/`produces` aimed recursively at the graph's own history (Check 3). Nyx's synthesis is deterritorialization/reterritorialization directly: Helios's, Selene's, and Eos's claims are broken from standing alone and reterritorialized onto `duskweave`, which none singly constituted. The council's `canonicalOrigin`, declared on top without erasing it (Check 4), is a further reterritorialization at one remove — at the scale the substratum relation actually fits: not four commits inflated into four geological-scale codings, but `sky/1`'s whole fact-layer as one stratum, Helios/Selene/Eos as its **epistrata**<sup>[pp. 50–52]</sup>, Nyx's synthesis and the council's declaration each a further stratum in the chain, checkable in `pantheon.rs`'s own log, no privileged top. Helios's, Selene's, and Eos's own commits remain, per Check 2, fully present and citable throughout — built upon, never captured.

The same holds for Datomic, event-sourced systems generally, and RDF under SPARQL — the same append-and-recombine shape, for the same reason. What such a system lacks structurally is the same triad: grounded citation against a real production history, not an internally-consistent-but-unverified store; connectivity crossing sovereign repositories with no shared authority (an event-sourced system is, ordinarily, one authority); and a default of non-convergence rather than an application layer that could enforce one. DMML's distinctiveness is that combination through an ordinary, unprivileged grammar primitive — not the shape alone, conceded widely, as the primary text's own claim to generality predicts.

D&G separate *relative* deterritorialization, always eventually reterritorialized, from *absolute*, tied to the plane of consistency's own "destratification" — and absolute is not the rarer case: "It is the strata that are always residue, not the opposite"<sup>[p. 56]</sup>; the relative/absolute difference is one of "nature," not speed or degree.<sup>[pp. 55–56]</sup> Nyx's synthesis and the council's declaration are both ordinary *relative* deterritorializations, each recodable by a further commit. Nothing here answers to *absolute* deterritorialization in D&G's stronger sense. The full commit log — never exhausted by any one stratifying read of it — is the closer, and on this corrected picture more natural, candidate for something plane-of-consistency-like at that scale: "the strata are spin-offs, thickenings on a plane of consistency that is everywhere, always primary and always immanent."<sup>[pp. 69–70]</sup> Establishing that precisely is further work left open, not claimed. This restraint is D&G's own recommended caution, not modesty: "Staying stratified—organized, signified, subjected—is not the worst that can happen; the worst that can happen is if you throw the strata into demented or suicidal collapse, which brings them back down on us heavier than ever."<sup>[verified 2026-08-25, "How Do You Make Yourself a Body without Organs?", p. 161]</sup> Their recommended practice is gradual and local — "lodge yourself on a stratum... have a small plot of new land at all times," "not with a sledgehammer" but "a very fine file"<sup>[pp. 160–161]</sup> — closer to Nyx's and the council's ordinary, relative moves than to anything this paper would call absolute.

What would count against this reading: if a future version of DMML's grammar forced convergence on `(subject, predicate)` pairs by default, the plane-of-consistency reading of the raw commit log would fail on its own terms and need retraction, not patching. If `consumes` could not cite more than one prior fact at once, double articulation would have nothing to point at. Neither holds today. A further failure condition: if a future substrate began enforcing declarations like `canonicalOrigin` at admission time — gating entry by conformance to a canonical layer rather than leaving it a revisable fact — the council's move would become the state's overcode in earnest, "substitut[ing] sets for the series."<sup>[p. 447]</sup> Section 1's admission gate today verifies genuineness at write time, not conformance to any canonical layer, so this condition is counterfactual — naming it is what keeps the extraction/stratic distinction from being merely definitional.

### Grounded reference, not signifiance

This bears on the rhizome subsection's own claim that DMML's citations are "verified productions, not claims that could be figurative." The **signifying regime** is fully self-consistent and still never escapes itself: "every sign refers to another sign, and only to another sign, ad infinitum," a network "infinitely circular," where "not much attention is paid to indexes... the territorial states of things constituting the designatable."<sup>[verified 2026-08-25, "On Several Regimes of Signs," pp. 112–113]</sup> Its problem is not inconsistency — every sign genuinely refers to a real prior sign, the same guarantee DMML's referential-integrity check provides — but that the chain never terminates outside itself. Since a DMML world's `produces` asserts triples into the same graph `consumes` cites from, and DMML has no sensor or extra-symbolic input channel, citing something "genuinely produced" risks meaning only citing another triple in the same closed graph — the signifying regime's own guarantee, not an escape from it.

The resolution is not that DMML's triples secretly refer to something non-symbolic — they don't. The graph is not causally sealed the way the signifying regime's chain is, and the site is `declare`: a predicate entering the vocabulary isn't derived from what's already there, it's introduced by an author's decision, and that decision — like every `consumes`/`produces` pair — comes from and returns to something outside the sign-chain: a player's action, an AI author's judgment resolving a petition, whatever prompted the commit, feeding back into a game an actual player experiences. The content stays symbolic throughout; its *causal embedding* does not. No canonical, first-class "sensor" primitive is required for this — authorship as an act performed by an agent in response to something is already the break, checkable the same way Section 3 already establishes for the canonical-answer question, from a different angle.

The same move answers a second concern: a closed, self-declared vocabulary looks like exactly what the same plateau calls an **axiomatic** operation — "subordinates [lines] to a punctual system," explicitly a diagram's *opposite*, "the program of a stratum, against the diagram of the plane of consistency."<sup>[p. 143]</sup> Fixed once and for all, it would earn that description; as `declare` actually makes it — closed only until the next author extends it, no central authority fixing the set in advance — it is closer to what this plateau's own discipline requires: real semiotic systems are **mixed**, not pure instances of one regime. "Perhaps all semiotics are mixed... Presignifying elements are always active in the signifying regime; countersignifying elements are always present and at work within it."<sup>[p. 119]</sup> DMML's vocabulary is exactly this mixture — axiomatic-shaped closure combined with diagrammatic-shaped opening — not a clean instance of either pole, for the same reason this paper has already declined clean-mergeable, clean-absolute, or clean-stratum readings elsewhere.

This is not a proof that DMML's petitions satisfy Simondon's transindividual after all, arrived at by strata instead of individuation. Simondon named something real that this section sets aside as the wrong primary register, not discards: D&G's own apparatus fits what DMML's grammar does — ongoing capture and release, never converging on a stable individual — more precisely than a concept built to name a determinate outcome. Whether DMML's stratification is a distant cousin of the transindividual after all, or something D&G's vocabulary was already reaching past it toward, is left open. A petition is one case of the general pattern, not a separate mechanism: Section 1's door/`room/9` lifecycle is a first articulation folded, by the resolving commit, into a second — a stable room, available afterward to be cited, recombined, and further stratified exactly as `pantheon.rs`'s facts are. That petitions needed no bespoke primitive to fit this reading is not, by itself, evidence the reading is right — cheap-to-fit is not distinctive of anything — but it does show the claim isn't special-cased to petitions: the reading was built and checked against `pantheon.rs`'s ordinary facts first.

### A second grounding: recombination of a recombination

`pantheon.rs`'s deities dispute an invented myth; every check above is real about the grammar but stakes nothing on any claim's actual content. A second, independently-built simulation — `dmml/examples/benjamin_full_essay.rs`, `benjamin_rival_reading.rs`, and `benjamin_second_reader.rs` — runs the same primitives against a real secondary text (Walter Benjamin's "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction") rather than an origin myth, and produces two structural points the pantheon simulation alone does not.

First, on **grounded reference**: the pantheon's vocabulary (`sunfire`, `moonwoven`, `duskweave`) is authored whole-cloth and checkable only against itself. The Benjamin graph's declared vocabulary — `aura`, `massDesire`, `distanceStrategy`, `simultaneousCollectiveCapacity` — is introduced the same way (`declare`, by authorial decision, closed only until extended), but each declaration is independently checkable against an actual external document none of the graph's authors wrote, verified separately before being committed.<sup>[verification method: direct primary-text read against the Harry Zohn translation, logged in this repository's own `papers/CITATION-VERIFICATION-2026-08-25-benjamin.md`]</sup> This does not change what Section 4 already argues about `declare`'s causal embedding — it gives that argument an instance where the "something outside the sign-chain" a declaration answers to is checkable by a third party, not asserted.

Second, and more consequential for the auto-recombinant claim itself: `pantheon.rs`'s Nyx recombines three *first-order* rival claims into a synthesis nothing in the inputs stated. `benjamin_rival_reading.rs` reproduces that shape once — a `did:plc:critical-reader` commit consumes the identical two facts an original commit's own loop-closure claim consumed, and produces an incompatible conclusion, the same evidence, read two ways — then a `disputes` commit consumes that rival claim directly and produces a counter-claim, both remaining independently real and citable, asignifying rupture applied to interpretive disagreement rather than to administrative provenance (`canonicalOrigin`). `benjamin_second_reader.rs` then goes one level further: its forced-regression commit does not consume either original claim — it consumes the *dispute*, citing the disputing commit's own `counterClaim` predicate specifically, and produces a claim that extends the dispute rather than re-litigating what it disputed. Recombination is here shown operating on the graph's own prior recombination-and-disagreement, not only on first-order facts — a stronger instance of "accumulated multiplicity as a first-class input to further production" than the pantheon simulation, by itself, established.

This subsection is itself an instance of what it describes: every claim above was written by materializing the three example files' own commit logs and reporting their checked `current_value` outputs, in the same register Section 4 elsewhere asks its claims to be read in, not composed and then illustrated after the fact.

## 5. The anti-Oedipal polemic does not transfer, and declining to force it is itself the argument

*Anti-Oedipus* is not a neutral metaphysics of production offered for general use. Its desiring-machines are introduced to dismantle a specific target: psychoanalysis's reduction of desire to the Oedipal family triangle, alongside capital's own reterritorialization of deterritorialized flows onto its own axiomatic. A world-modeling grammar has no unconscious to reduce to a family structure, and this paper does not manufacture a hidden anti-Oedipal argument where none exists.

DMML's closed, structural vocabulary contains no primitive representing lack, want, or need as foundational; anything of that shape has to be authored as ordinary, non-privileged, self-declared content, on the same footing as any other predicate. One genuine question sits adjacent to the polemic without being it: whether self-declared predicates, once introduced, tend over time to get absorbed into fixed convention across many authors and worlds — a small-scale, empirical version of the axiomatic-capture question.

Two authors independently declared self-declared predicates that are not part of ordinary English vocabulary, and this convergence is the sharpest evidence available on that question — one that otherwise cannot be answered without evidence of actual usage patterns that does not yet exist. That evidence already exists in this repository's own 24 independently-motivated DMML example files, accumulated across a single session with two genuinely separate authors — a dispatched adversarial model and a separate agent given only primary-text access, neither instructed on vocabulary — making this available evidence for the question rather than a study still to be run. Across 271 declare-attribute statements in those 24 files, 84 distinct predicate names appear, with "claim" alone accounting for 91 of them; both of the genuinely independent-author files declared "claim" too, and both also independently declared "counterClaim," "distanceStrategy," and "vocabularyStance" without being instructed to reuse those specific names. The convergence on "claim" is weak evidence, since it is a generic English word any author modeling an assertion would reach for regardless of any convention-forming pressure; the sharper evidence is the convergence on task-specific coinages like "counterClaim," which are absent from ordinary vocabulary and were arrived at by two authors who never coordinated and were not told to reuse them. Even so, the defensible claim this data supports is narrower than it might first appear: task-specific self-declared predicates show real convergence pressure under dispatch conditions, where an author given an existing graph's vocabulary in context tends to reuse its exact names rather than rephrase them — which is evidence of citation-name stability under shared context, not yet evidence of spontaneous convergence between authors with zero shared context, and the stronger claim the open question actually asks about remains untested by this dataset.

This paragraph is itself a materialization, not composed prose fitted to citations after the fact: it was produced by a dispatched editor given only five checked `(subject, predicate, value)` facts from `dmml/examples/paper_predicate_convergence.rs`'s own commit graph — never the pre-existing prose this replaces, never this paper's own framing — under a standing rule that every claim in its output has to trace to one of those five facts (`.claude/agents/materialization-editor.md`). The editor's own traceability note confirmed each sentence's source fact and that every hedge above ("not yet evidence," "remains untested") was preserved exactly as the graph stated it, not sharpened or softened in the retelling.

### Open critical debts, surfaced by autoregressive dispatch

Three fresh readers were dispatched in sequence against this paper's own text — the same role each time, each round also given every prior round's output with an explicit instruction not to repeat it — to test whether repeated dispatch against a fixed but growing body of material converges (later rounds restate earlier ones) or generates (later rounds keep finding real, distinct content). Checked directly in `dmml/examples/autoregressive_critique.rs`: all three rounds triangulated a different pair of the paper's own claims, none cited another round's output rather than the paper itself, and all three remain independently real in the combined log. Three rounds is not enough to establish this as a general property of the dispatch method, but for this run it generated rather than converged, and the three critiques are real enough to owe a direct answer rather than a citation alone.

**First**, Section 1's fail-open citation semantics (`fact_retraction_fails_open` — a `consumes` entry naming a fabricated fact is accepted, not rejected) undercuts the auto-recombinant claim itself: nothing structurally distinguishes a commit that legitimately synthesizes real prior facts from one that cites the same facts and produces something with no defensible relation to them. Both are "grounded" in exactly the sense Section 4 requires. Auto-recombinant may be a property of the well-behaved subset of commits this paper's own examples happen to contain, not a structural property the grammar itself enforces or even nudges toward — a live possibility this paper has not ruled out.

**Second**, Section 4's non-convergence claim (nothing forces convergence on facts) sits in tension with Section 5's own convergence finding (independent authors do converge, on predicate vocabulary, under the shared-context dispatch conditions real multi-author DMML worlds actually run under, since later commits can always see earlier ones). If schema-level convergence is real and shared-context-driven, the same pressure has no principled stopping point short of eventually producing convergence on which facts count as canonical too — meaning the auto-recombinant, non-convergent multiplicity Section 4 treats as DMML's distinguishing structural property may be a transient phase awaiting exactly the convergence pressure Section 5 already measured, not a stable one.

**Third, and sharpest**: Section 5's convergence result does not survive its own paper's evidentiary standard. Section 3 explicitly worries that petition resolution, sampled from a learned distribution, might be "selection from an already-determined menu" rather than production — and declines to resolve whether it is. Section 5's own instrument for its most interesting sociological claim is two LLM-dispatched authoring agents converging on predicate names, conditioned on overlapping training distributions and overlapping context — the identical structure Section 3 flags as suspect, applied here without the same suspicion. Two LLMs converging on `distanceStrategy` may be two samples from one distribution converging on its mode — true of ordinary code review or ontology engineering generally, and not a claim about DMML specifically — and nothing in Section 5 controls for it (no human-authored comparison, no unrelated-domain comparison). This paper's own standard, applied evenly, would require the same hedge here that Section 3 already applies to the resolver, and Section 5 as written does not carry it.

None of the three is patched here. Patching them individually risks exactly the "plausible-sounding claim unsupported by data" this paper elsewhere declines to reach for — Section 4's response to the first two would need either a real distinguishing mechanism this paper hasn't found or an honest concession that auto-recombinance is authorial-convention-dependent, not grammar-enforced; the third needs an actual control condition this paper does not yet have. They stand as open debts, checkable against the commit log that raised them, in the same register this paper asks every other claim to be read in.

## 6. What this is not claiming

DMML is not offered as a psychoanalytic theory, a claim about human desire, or evidence of anything resembling consciousness. The correspondence argued here is structural and checkable at the level of the grammar (Section 3); extended to strata, deterritorialization, and the plane of consistency (Section 4), it is a direction, checked against a real, runnable simulation rather than asserted, not a proof. A production model borrowed at the level of one specific structural fact (no canonical-answer function) is a real structural correspondence, not an inheritance of either theory's full ontological weight.
