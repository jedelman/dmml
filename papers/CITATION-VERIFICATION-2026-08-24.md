## Claim 1 — Anti-Oedipus, desiring-machines, breast-machine/mouth-machine, "desire produces the real"

**Claim:** *Anti-Oedipus* never gives a tidy dictionary definition of the
desiring-machine — it arrives at the concept through examples
(breast-machine, mouth-machine) and the general thesis that desire produces
the real rather than lacking something.

**Grade: ✅ Verified**

**Sources found:**
- Multiple close-reading summaries of *Anti-Oedipus* ch. 1 confirm the
  book opens (section 1, "The Desiring-Machines") with concrete machine
  examples rather than a definition: "the breast is a machine that produces
  milk, and the mouth is a machine coupled to it," with the mouth-machine
  further described as multifunctional (eating, speaking, breathing,
  etc.). See the Anti-Oedipus Project reading-group series (Noah
  Christiansen, Medium, "Anti-Oedipus 1.1: Desiring-Production" and "1.5:
  The Machines") and the freely available full English text (libcom.org
  mirror of the Robert Hurley/Mark Seem/Helen R. Lane translation,
  Viking/University of Minnesota Press).
- The book's opening line — "Everywhere it is machines — real ones, not
  figurative ones" — is widely and consistently quoted across these
  sources in the same form, indicating it is the real opening move of the
  text, not a paraphrase drifting from it.
- The "desire produces the real" thesis is likewise well attested: "If
  desire produces, it produces the real" and the explicit anti-lack
  framing ("desire is not to be identified with lack... but rather with
  production") both appear as direct paraphrase/quotation across
  independent secondary sources (williamwatkin.blogspot.com's chapter
  notes, the Anti-Oedipus Project series, "An Un-canny Ontology").

**Assessment:** Both halves of the claim hold up: the examples-not-definition
structure and the production-not-lack thesis are both accurately stated at
the level of generality the outline uses. For drafting, the paper should
still pull exact page/section numbers from a specific edition (Minnesota
Press pagination is the standard one to cite) rather than relying on the
secondary retellings used here for this pass — this search located and
corroborated the *content* of the claim, not a citable pincite.

## Claim 2 — Simondon, transindividual, *L'individuation psychique et collective*, replacing "intersubjectivity"

**Claim:** Simondon's "transindividual" is his deliberate replacement for
"intersubjectivity," requiring participants to be individuated *through*
the relation itself (not just producing a shared artifact together),
laid out specifically in *L'individuation psychique et collective* (as
distinct from *L'individuation à la lumière des notions de forme et
d'information*).

**Grade: ✅ Verified** (with one nuance noted below)

**Sources found:**
- *L'individuation psychique et collective* is confirmed as a real,
  correctly titled Simondon work (1989), the second part of his doctoral
  thesis, following *L'individu et sa genèse physico-biologique* (1964) —
  confirmed via Amazon.fr's listing of the actual French edition and
  Goodreads' catalog entry, both showing the exact title. The
  transindividual concept is specifically discussed as central to this
  text (per the Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews review of Simondon's
  *Psychic and Collective Individuation*, and multiple summaries of
  Combes).
- Muriel Combes' *Gilbert Simondon and the Philosophy of the
  Transindividual* is confirmed real (MIT Press, "Technologies of Lived
  Abstraction" series; ISBN 9780262018180; reviewed at Notre Dame
  Philosophical Reviews and PhilPapers).
- The NDPR review of Combes directly supports the "replaces
  intersubjectivity" / "individuated through the relation" framing: it
  states the transindividual is "not purely inter-individual — at least
  in the manner phenomenology has defined 'inter-individuality,'" and
  that what defines the domain "are not the substances filling it, but
  the functions born of the individuating doubling" — i.e., individuals
  are constituted by/through the relation, not merely joined by an
  external, already-individuated act. This is a real, accurately
  summarized piece of secondary scholarship, not an invented framing.

**Nuance:** the NDPR review of Combes attributes the core transindividual
metaphysics across *both* major Simondon works — the doctoral thesis as a
whole, with *L'individuation psychique et collective* specifically doing
the ethical/political extension of concepts partly laid down in
*L'individuation à la lumière des notions de forme et d'information*
material. The outline's clean division ("transindividual lives in the
1989 book; form/information/metastability lives in the other") is *broadly*
right and commonly used as shorthand, but scholarship treats the two as
one continuous thesis rather than two hermetically separate arguments — the
paper should soften "specifically" to something like "developed at length
in" rather than implying the concept appears nowhere else in Simondon's
work.

## Claim 3 — "World Models" (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018), VAE+RNN, foundational framing

**Claim:** A well-known, foundational 2018 paper titled "World Models" by
David Ha and Jürgen Schmidhuber, using a VAE+RNN architecture, is usually
credited as establishing the "world models" framing in ML/RL.

**Grade: ✅ Verified**

**Source:** arXiv:1803.10122, "World Models," David Ha and Jürgen
Schmidhuber (2018) — confirmed directly via the arXiv abstract page
(arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122). Abstract confirms the paper builds
"generative neural network models of popular reinforcement learning
environments," trains a compact policy on features extracted from the
learned world model, and demonstrates training an agent "entirely inside
of its own hallucinated dream generated by its world model." The
VAE-for-compressed-spatial-representation + RNN-for-temporal/predictive
dynamics architecture is consistent with the paper's stated method
(unsupervised learning of a "compressed spatial and temporal
representation of the environment") and is exactly the description
repeated across independent secondary sources (paper-reading-group
write-ups, Medium explainer). Note the arXiv identifier is exact
(1803.10122) — worth citing directly rather than through a secondary
retelling.

## Claim 4 — Genie / Genie 2 / Genie 3 (Google DeepMind), "generative interactive environments," action-controllable

**Claim:** A real system called "Genie" (or "Genie 2"/"Genie 3") from
Google DeepMind, a "generative interactive environment(s)" model — a world
model trained to produce playable/controllable video-game-like
environments, action-conditioned.

**Grade: ✅ Verified**

**Source:** Google DeepMind's own blog post, "Genie 3: A new frontier for
world models" (deepmind.google/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/),
fetched directly. DeepMind's own language: Genie 3 is "a general purpose
world model that can generate an unprecedented diversity of interactive
environments," navigable "in real time at 24 frames per second," with
consistency retained "for a few minutes" at 720p. It is explicitly
described as action-controllable: users navigate via directional
controls and can trigger "promptable world events" that modify the
generated world in real time. Announced by Google DeepMind on August 5,
2025. Genie 1 and 2 (2024) are confirmed as real predecessors that could
generate environments for agents but lacked Genie 3's real-time
interactivity.

**Assessment:** "action-controllable"/"playable" is *not* an overstatement
here — it is DeepMind's own stated framing, not press exaggeration. If the
paper wants a second, even more explicitly research-paper-shaped source
(rather than the blog post), the original Genie 1 paper ("Genie:
Generative Interactive Environments," 2024) should be pulled and cited
separately, since the outline currently treats "Genie" as one system
across three iterations when DeepMind treats them as a numbered sequence
with a real capability jump specifically at Genie 3 (first real-time
version).

## Claim 5 — Compositional generalization in the world-models literature

**Claim (open-ended):** does the ML world-models literature (Ha/Schmidhuber
lineage through Genie-style systems) explicitly discuss "compositional
generalization" as a property distinguishing learned latent world models
from symbolic/discrete ones?

**Grade: ⚠️ Plausible, unverified as stated — but real adjacent literature exists**

**Findings:** There is a real, active body of work using the exact phrase
"compositional generalization" in the world-models context — e.g. "Toward
Compositional Generalization in Object-Oriented World Modeling" (Zhao,
Kong et al., via Semantic Scholar), "PoE-World: Compositional World
Modeling with Products of Programmatic Experts" (arXiv:2505.10819),
"Inter-environmental world modeling for continuous and compositional
dynamics" (arXiv:2503.09911), and related work on object-centric
representations and identifiability theory showing when autoencoders
"provably generalize compositionally." This confirms the *topic* is a
real, live concern in the field.

**What was not found:** none of these papers trace back to the original
Ha/Schmidhuber 2018 paper or to the Genie line specifically framing
compositional generalization as *the* symbolic-vs-latent dividing line the
outline's section 4 wants. The connection the outline wants to draw
(discrete/symbolic vocabularies compose "by construction" vs. latent
models' generalization behavior) is a reasonable characterization of *why*
researchers pursue compositional world models, but it is this paper's own
synthesis, not something a cited ML paper states in those terms. Grading
this ⚠️ rather than ✅: don't claim a source says "compositional
generalization is what distinguishes symbolic from latent world models" —
no such single explicit statement was located. Cite the PoE-World /
object-oriented-world-modeling line as evidence that compositionality is a
recognized *gap/goal* in latent models, and let the paper make its own
argument about what that implies for DMML, clearly flagged as the paper's
own comparison rather than an existing citation.
