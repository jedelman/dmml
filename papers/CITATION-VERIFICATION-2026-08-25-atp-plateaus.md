# Citation Verification Pass — Four Further ATP Plateaus (2026-08-25)

Deep primary-text read (full OCR text of the Massumi translation, page-cited
from running headers) of four plateaus beyond the two already used
("Rhizome," "Geology of Morals"), plus independent re-confirmation of the
substratum relativity correction. Grades use the project's standard scale.

## 1. "1440: The Smooth and the Striated" (pp. 474–500) — ✅ Full primary text read

Smooth/striated exist only in mixture, never as a clean binary: "the two
spaces in fact exist only in mixture" (p. 474–475). Patchwork as the figure
for non-hierarchical, joinable-in-many-ways composition: "An amorphous
collection of juxtaposed pieces that can be joined together in an infinite
number of ways... patchwork is literally a Riemannian space" (p. 476–477);
quilts specifically: "it is possible for there to be no top or bottom"
(p. 476). Points subordinated to trajectory, not vice versa, in smooth space
(p. 478); "Smooth space is filled by events or haecceities... haptic rather
than optical" (p. 479). Honest anti-triumphalist hedge, worth citing on its
own: "Never believe that a smooth space will suffice to save us" (p. 500).
Adds real precision beyond rhizome/tree for DMML's "no forced convergence"
claim: gives a positive account (patchwork, no top/bottom) rather than only
a negative one (not a tree).

## 2. "7000 B.C.: Apparatus of Capture" (pp. 424–473) — ✅ Full primary text read, real disanalogy flagged

Capture defined as inserting a comparison/equivalence mechanism between
already-existing series: "the mechanism of capture contributes from the
outset to the constitution of the aggregate upon which the capture is
effectuated" (p. 447). Rent, profit, tax named explicitly as parallel
apparatuses of capture (p. 442); "surplus value of code" language confirmed
real (p. 451). **Important disanalogy**: D&G's capture is unambiguously
extractive and hierarchy-producing (rent taps profit, tax extorts), not a
neutral verification mechanism — their whole analytic point is that capture
*is* the extraction. Using "capture" for DMML's referential-integrity check
would need the extractive content explicitly stripped and flagged as a
structural-only borrowing, or the term avoided. Not integrated into either
paper this round given the risk of overclaiming.

## 3. "November 28, 1947: How Do You Make Yourself a Body without Organs?" (pp. 149–166) — ✅ Full primary text read, strong

Explicit warning against reckless total destratification, and explicit
statement that staying stratified is not the worst outcome: "Staying
stratified—organized, signified, subjected—is not the worst that can
happen; the worst that can happen is if you throw the strata into demented
or suicidal collapse, which brings them back down on us heavier than ever"
(p. 161). Positive alternative: "Lodge yourself on a stratum, experiment
with the opportunities it offers... have a small plot of new land at all
times" (p. 161); "Caution is the art... You don't do it with a sledgehammer,
you use a very fine file" (p. 160). Directly supports, on D&G's own
authority, that declining to claim absolute deterritorialization is the
correct caution, not a modest fallback.

## 4. "1837: Of the Refrain" (pp. 310–350) — ✅ Substantial primary text read

Territory is constituted by marking, not geological layering: "The
territory is not primary in relation to the qualitative mark; it is the
mark that makes the territory" (p. 315); "Territorialization is an act of
rhythm that has become expressive" (p. 315). Territory ≠ stratum: "we do
not yet have a Territory, which is not a milieu... The territory is in fact
an act that affects milieus and rhythms, that 'territorializes' them"
(p. 314). Refrain (*ritournelle*) defined: "any aggregate of matters of
expression that draws a territory and develops into territorial motifs"
(p. 323); territorial marking by song: "Bird songs: the bird sings to mark
its territory" (p. 312). Territory inherently subject to deterritorialization
from within: "A territory is always en route to an at least potential
deterritorialization" (p. 320–321). Genuinely more precise fit than "stratum"
for a DMML world's persistent, self-declared predicate vocabulary — marking/
expression, not physical layering.

## 5. Substratum relativity — independently reconfirmed, ✅ with the same correction

"Each stratum serves as the substratum for another stratum" (p. 73); no
fixed hierarchy — substrata are "no less complex than, nor... inferior to"
strata (p. 49); "there is no fixed order, and one stratum can serve directly
as a substratum for another... there is no lesser, no higher or lower,
organization" (p. 69–70); internal doubling — "Epistrata and parastrata must
themselves be thought of as strata" (p. 73), "each stratum is double (it
itself has several layers)" (p. 45). **"Superstratum" confirmed absent**
from the full-text search a second time, independently — D&G's term is
"substratum," never a coined counterpart. Territory (from "Of the Refrain")
is explicitly a *different* concept from stratum, not a further stratum in
the same relativity chain — the two vocabularies should not be conflated.
